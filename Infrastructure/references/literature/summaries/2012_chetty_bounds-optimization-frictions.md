# Chetty (2012) — Bounds on Elasticities with Optimization Frictions

- **Tier:** 1 (peer-reviewed, Econometrica)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.3982/ECTA9043
- **Source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA9043 ; NBER w15616
- **Thread:** A (elasticity consensus anchor)
- **Evidence class:** Synthesis of measured/ex-post micro estimates with bounds methodology.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Derives bounds on structural price elasticities as a function of the observed
response, the size of the price change, and the degree of optimization frictions
(adjustment costs, inattention). Frictions attenuate intensive-margin elasticities
more than extensive-margin ones. After accounting for frictions, reports a Hicksian
(steady-state) labor supply elasticity of **0.33 on the intensive margin** and
**0.25 on the extensive margin**. Small frictions can reconcile micro vs. macro and
intensive vs. extensive disparities.

## Why it matters for 80-80
These are the canonical "consensus" steady-state elasticity magnitudes a dynamic
cost model can adopt as a central case. The extensive-margin figure (~0.25 Hicksian)
is the load-bearing parameter for an entry-into-work transfer. The frictions framing
also warns that short-run responses to a newly introduced subsidy may be smaller than
the long-run structural elasticity.
