## Finding (paraphrased)
The canonical statement of the modern search-matching model of equilibrium unemployment.
A matching function turns unemployed workers and vacancies into new matches. Each match
produces output that, net of the worker's value of non-employment, generates a **match
surplus**; the wage divides that surplus by Nash bargaining. Two equilibrium conditions
define the model:

1. **Job-creation / free-entry (vacancy) condition.** Firms post vacancies until the
   expected discounted value of a filled job equals the cost of posting. With free entry,
   the value of a vacancy is driven to zero, which (together with the wage rule) pins down
   market tightness theta = v/u and the job-finding rate.
2. **Surplus / viability condition.** A match forms (or survives) only while its surplus
   is non-negative: idiosyncratic productivity shocks drive surplus below zero at a
   reservation-productivity threshold, triggering separation (job destruction). The
   structural object is S = J + W - V - U; a match is viable iff S >= 0, i.e. iff match
   output y covers the worker's outside option r.

The case where the relevant productivity falls below the reservation threshold (surplus
< 0) is exactly the "structurally non-employed" object the 80-80 model needs: a worker
whose reservation value r exceeds attainable output y forms no viable match.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Supplies both equations the structural simulation rests on: (a) the **viability
condition** S >= 0 <=> y - r >= 0, which classifies who is on the margin of employment and
how a subsidy that adds s to joint surplus (y + s - r) can make previously non-viable
matches viable (the employment-creation channel); and (b) the **job-creation condition**
that determines how many such matches actually form in equilibrium once firms re-optimize
vacancy posting. The subsidy enters the surplus, shifts the reservation-productivity
threshold, and is split by bargaining — the micro-foundation for both the employment and
the incidence results.
