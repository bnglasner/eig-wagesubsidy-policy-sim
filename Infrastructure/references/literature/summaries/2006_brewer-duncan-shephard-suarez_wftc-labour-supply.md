## Finding (verified from the final-evaluation abstract, read directly)
Quoting the abstract: "Estimates suggest that by 2002, WFTC had increased labour supply of
**lone mothers by around 5.11 percentage points**, slightly **reduced labour supply of
mothers in couples by 0.57 percentage points**, and **increased the labour supply of
fathers in couples by 0.75 percentage points**, compared with the benefit that preceded it,
called Family Credit. In aggregate, these changes are equivalent to a fall of 99,000 in the
number of workless families with children, and a net increase in labour market
participation of 81,000 workers." Contemporaneous tax/benefit reforms acted to *reduce*
parents' labour supply, so the overall post-1999 package effect is lower. The utility cost
of programme participation fell under WFTC for lone mothers (raising their take-up and
entry) but was unchanged for couples.

`[unverified: the Labour Economics 13(6):699-720 published version's point estimates were
not read from the paywalled journal text; one secondary search summary attributes a
smaller -0.2pp mothers-in-couples figure to the journal version. The numbers quoted above
are pinned to the directly-read HMRC final-evaluation abstract (2005). Treat -0.57pp /
-0.2pp as the same qualitative finding at slightly different vintages of the model.]`

## Why it matters for the `other_women` band
This is the cleanest quantified statement of the **second-earner disincentive under a
household-income-assessed credit**: partnered mothers' participation falls, but only by
about **half a percentage point** — an order of magnitude smaller than lone mothers' +5.11pp
gain, and small even relative to fathers' +0.75pp gain. Confirms the sign and bounds the
size of the household-testing penalty in a real, mature program (the UK analog of
Eissa-Hoynes' ~1pp U.S. EITC result). Critically for the 80-80: this negative operates
through WFTC's **joint family income test**, a design feature the individually-assessed
80-80 does not have. What remains for the 80-80 is only the smaller intra-household
income-effect channel (a partner's subsidy raising household income). So the appropriate
transfer to the `other_women` band is: (a) the *sign risk* is real but (b) even under the
WORST design for second earners (a full household means test), the measured drag is
roughly -0.5 to -1pp of participation — a floor-setting, not a central, consideration.

## Caveats
- UK institutional context (Family Credit baseline, 16-hour minimum work condition);
  magnitudes are design-specific.
- "Mothers in couples," not all partnered women: WFTC required children; childless
  partnered women are outside the sample, as in all EITC/WFTC evidence.
- Structural model results, contingent on the discrete-choice specification; but estimated
  across the actual reform rather than simulated from pre-reform parameters alone
  (upgrades on Blundell et al. 2000, which projected the same qualitative pattern ex ante).
