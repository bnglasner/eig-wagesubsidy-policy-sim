## Finding (paraphrased)
Derives the condition under which decentralized search equilibrium is socially efficient:
the **Hosios condition** states that the worker's bargaining share beta must equal the
elasticity of the matching function with respect to unemployment (the worker's
contribution to match creation). When beta exceeds that elasticity, too few vacancies are
posted (over-reward to workers); when below, too many. This is the theoretical anchor for
choosing beta in calibrated DMP models: with a symmetric Cobb-Douglas matching function of
elasticity 0.5, the efficient beta is 0.5 — the value most DMP calibrations adopt.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Supplies the **focal central value beta = 0.5** for the incidence module (efficient
surplus split under a symmetric matching function), and the principled reason it is the
default rather than an arbitrary choice. Frames the bargaining-share parameter as the
single object that governs how a subsidy's surplus is divided between worker and firm.
