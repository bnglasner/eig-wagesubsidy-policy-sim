# Hall & Milgrom (2008) — The Limited Influence of Unemployment on the Wage Bargain

- **Tier:** 1 (American Economic Review)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/aer.98.4.1653
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.98.4.1653 ; AER 98(4):1653-1674
- **Thread:** 2/3 (wage determination and bargaining; how strongly the outside option r enters the wage).
- **Evidence class:** Modeled (theory) with quantitative calibration.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Replaces standard Nash bargaining (where the threat point is the value of unemployment)
with an **alternating-offers / delay-cost** bargaining protocol. The key result: the
bargained wage depends on the parties' **costs of delaying agreement**, not primarily on
the worker's outside option (the value of non-employment). Consequence: the wage responds
much **less** to changes in unemployment / the outside option than the textbook Nash rule
implies — i.e., wages are more rigid with respect to r. This dampened pass-through of the
outside option to the wage is what lets the model generate realistic employment volatility
(addressing the Shimer puzzle).

## Why it matters for 80-80 (important caveat for the incidence module)
The baseline 80-80 incidence rule, w = r + beta*(y - r), assumes the wage moves
one-for-one with beta times changes in surplus, so a subsidy s is split beta/(1-beta).
Hall-Milgrom is the principal **caution**: if wage setting is governed by delay costs
rather than the outside option, the wage (and hence the worker's captured share of a
subsidy) may respond **less** than the simple Nash split predicts. This argues for treating
the Nash split as one end of a range and considering a more wage-rigid alternative in which
the firm captures more of s in the short run. Pairs with Cahuc-Carcillo-Le Barbanchon
(2019) and Benzarti-Harju (2021) (both in catalog) on slack/rigidity-dependent pass-through.
