# Benzarti & Harju (2021) — Can Payroll Tax Cuts Help Firms During Recessions?

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal of Public Economics)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2021.104472
- **Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0047272721000967 ; DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2021.104472 (NBER w27485 working-paper version)
- **Thread:** B angle 3 (incidence & wage pass-through) — measured evidence on how payroll-tax changes split between wages and employment, and how that split depends on the macro state.
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post) quasi-experimental study of a Finnish regional payroll-tax cut.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Uses a Finnish regional payroll-tax cut (and its later repeal) as a natural experiment.
Findings:
- In **normal times**, the payroll-tax cut is **passed through to higher wages** rather than
  to more employment — workers capture the cut, employment changes little.
- During a **recession**, firms instead use the payroll-tax cut to **support employment and
  survival** (the wage pass-through weakens). The benefit of a payroll-tax cut is therefore
  **state-dependent**: wage-incidence in good times, employment-incidence in bad times.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Third measured data point (with Busso et al. 2013 and Cahuc et al. 2019) for the same
lesson from a different country/instrument: **labor-market slack determines whether a
wage-linked subsidy raises wages or employment.** For the 80-80 incidence module, it
supports treating wage pass-through / employer capture as a function of slack rather than a
single constant, and it implies that in tight labor markets a worker-side subsidy is more
likely to be retained in wages (favorable for workers) while in recessions it does more on
the employment margin.

- **Scope caveat:** Employer-side payroll-tax cut, Finland; symmetry to a worker-side wage
  fill is a theoretical extension.
