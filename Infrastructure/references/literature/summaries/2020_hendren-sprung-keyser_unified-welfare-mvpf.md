# Hendren & Sprung-Keyser (2020) — A Unified Welfare Analysis of Government Policies

- **DOI:** 10.1093/qje/qjaa006
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/135/3/1209/5781614 ; NBER w26144
- **Thread:** B angle 2 (MVPF / welfare framing) — the central reference.
- **Evidence class:** Synthesis of measured causal estimates into an ex-ante welfare metric (MVPF).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Builds the Marginal Value of Public Funds (MVPF = beneficiaries' willingness to pay /
net government cost inclusive of fiscal externalities) for 133 historical US policy
changes. Net cost incorporates long-run effects on the government budget — i.e.,
policies that induce earnings can partly **pay for themselves**. Finds direct cash
transfers to adults tend to have lower MVPFs than investments in low-income children,
which can have MVPFs that are effectively infinite (pay for themselves).

## Why it matters for 80-80
Provides the welfare accounting framework for the 80-80: the right metric is not gross
budget cost but net cost after behavioral fiscal externalities (induced tax payments,
reduced transfers), divided by recipients' willingness to pay. Directly operationalizes
the project's "net of tax recapture and safety-net responses" question.
