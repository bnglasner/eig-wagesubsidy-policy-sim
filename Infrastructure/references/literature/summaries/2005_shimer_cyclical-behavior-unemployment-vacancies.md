## Finding (paraphrased)
The benchmark quantitative evaluation of the standard DMP model. Shimer calibrates the
search-matching model to US data and shows that, under conventional parameters including
**worker bargaining share beta = 0.72 set to satisfy the Hosios condition** for his
matching-function elasticity, the model generates far too little volatility in
unemployment and vacancies relative to the data (the "Shimer puzzle"). The relevant point
for the 80-80 is the **calibration convention**: beta is routinely chosen either at 0.5
(symmetric) or via Hosios to match the matching-function elasticity, and Shimer's headline
calibration uses beta ~ 0.72. This brackets the plausible range and shows where the field
sets the surplus-split parameter in disciplined quantitative work.

## Why it matters for 80-80
A second, quantitatively disciplined anchor for beta. Combined with Hosios (1990, beta =
0.5 under symmetry) it supports a **central beta near 0.5-0.7 with a sensitivity band**,
and shows that the standard practice is to tie beta to the matching elasticity rather than
free-estimate it. Also documents how the job-creation/free-entry condition responds to
productivity — useful for the employment-creation side of the simulation.
