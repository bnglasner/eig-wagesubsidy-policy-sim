## Finding (paraphrased)
Authoritative JEL survey of search-theoretic labor-market models. Lays out the common
architecture: frictional meeting (matching function), a match-specific surplus equal to
output net of the parties' outside options, and a wage-determination rule (Nash
bargaining, wage posting, or alternating offers) that divides that surplus. Distinguishes
the leading wage protocols and their efficiency properties, including the Hosios condition
under Nash bargaining. Provides the map for which modeling choices (bargaining vs. posting,
exogenous vs. endogenous separation) matter for policy analysis.

## Why it matters for 80-80
The reference that justifies the specific modeling choices the 80-80 simulation makes:
adopting Nash bargaining over the match surplus (rather than wage posting), and treating
the wage as a surplus split governed by beta. Cite for the framework-selection rationale
and for situating the incidence mechanism within the broader search-model family.
