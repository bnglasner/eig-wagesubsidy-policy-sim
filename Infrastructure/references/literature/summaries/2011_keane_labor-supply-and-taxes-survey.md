# Keane (2011) — Labor Supply and Taxes: A Survey

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal of Economic Literature)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/jel.49.4.961
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.49.4.961 ; gattonweb.uky.edu PDF
- **Thread:** A (elasticity survey anchor)
- **Evidence class:** Survey of measured estimates; argues for larger elasticities.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Comprehensive survey of labor-supply elasticity estimates and methods. Simple
average of **Hicks elasticities ~0.31** across studies. Frisch elasticities relevant
to fiscal analysis range **0.27 to 0.53, central ~0.40**. Argues that conventional
estimates understate true responsiveness once human-capital and dynamic effects
(experience, fertility, education) are included, especially for women on the
participation margin — i.e., long-run elasticities can be substantially larger.

## Conflict note
Keane sits on the **higher** end of the elasticity debate; contrasts with the smaller
steady-state values emphasized by Chetty (2012) and the CBO central estimate (0.19).
The cost model should treat Keane as the upper-bound anchor in a sensitivity range.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Defines the high end of the plausible elasticity range and warns that static
short-run elasticities may understate long-run entry/earnings responses to a
permanent subsidy.
