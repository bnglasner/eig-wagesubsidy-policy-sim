# Hall & Mueller (2018) — Wage Dispersion and Search Behavior: The Importance of Nonwage Job Values

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal of Political Economy)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1086/697739
- **Source:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/697739 ; NBER w21764
- **Thread:** 4 (reservation-wage determinants, calibrating r_i) / entry-margin reality-anchoring pass (2026-07-09)
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post) + structural. Uses the same Krueger-Mueller New Jersey UI-recipient survey data (offered wages, reservation wages, acceptance decisions) to identify a search model.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Identifies, from unemployed jobseekers' actual acceptance/rejection decisions, the joint
distributions of offered wages, offered NONWAGE job values, the value of the jobseeker's
non-work alternative, and personal productivity:
- Conditional on personal productivity, the standard deviation of offered log wages is
  **0.24**, while the dispersion of the **nonwage component of offered job values is larger,
  0.34**; the resulting dispersion of offered job values is 0.38. Dispersion of personal
  productivity is high (**0.43**).
- Jobseekers **frequently accept jobs paying less than their previously stated reservation
  wage**, and less frequently reject jobs paying more than it; **about two-thirds of offer
  rejections are motivated by nonwage reasons** (hours, location, conditions), not the wage.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Q1: how far above market wages do reservation values sit?)
Two implications for the model's calibrated reservation markups:
1. The behaviorally relevant object is the **value of the whole job (wage + nonwage)**
  relative to the value of the non-work alternative — not the wage alone. A calibrated
  "reservation markup over own market wage" partly proxies for nonwage job values and
  non-work option values, so very large implied wage-multiples for some groups are not
  per se absurd; they bundle nonwage components a wage-only fill cannot buy.
2. Symmetrically, because ~2/3 of rejections are nonwage-motivated, a **pure wage subsidy
  moves only the wage argument** of the job-value comparison — supporting entry responses
  well below what a wage-gap-closure calculation alone would suggest.
Note the population is **UI-recipient unemployed** — no direct NILF reservation-value
measurement exists here; extrapolation of these distributions to the NILF pool is a modeling
assumption, not a measured fact.
