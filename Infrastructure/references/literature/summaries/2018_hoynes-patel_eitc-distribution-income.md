# Hoynes & Patel (2018) — Effective Policy for Reducing Poverty and Inequality? (EITC)

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal of Human Resources)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.3368/jhr.53.4.1115.7494R1
- **Source:** https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/53/4/859 ; NBER w21340
- **Thread:** A (program-specific EITC, extensive margin) + Thread B angle 1 (earnings feedback to net cost)
- **Evidence class:** Measured/ex-post, quasi-experimental (variation by tax year and family size).

## Finding (paraphrased)
A **policy-induced $1,000 increase in the EITC raises employment by ~7.3 percentage
points** and cuts the share of affected families below 100% of poverty by ~9.4 pp.
Income gains concentrate between 75% and 150% of poverty. Crucially: static antipoverty
calculations that ignore the induced earnings effect **understate the EITC's
antipoverty impact by almost 50 percent**.

## Why it matters for 80-80
The $1,000 -> 7.3pp employment semi-elasticity is a directly usable extensive-margin
input. The "static understates by ~50%" result is the core argument for *dynamic*
cost/benefit modeling of the 80-80 fill: behavioral earnings change both the benefit
distribution and the net fiscal cost.
