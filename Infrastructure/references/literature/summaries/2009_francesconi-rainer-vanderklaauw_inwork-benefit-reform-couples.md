## Finding (verified from abstract + corroborating secondary summaries)
Examines WFTC's effects on couples with a household-bargaining model and BHPS panel data.
Core results:

- The reform's financial incentives had "**small and statistically insignificant effects
  on a wide range of married mothers' decisions**" on average.
- But "women's responses ... were highly heterogeneous, depending on their partners'
  labour supply and earnings": the employment effect is **insignificant for married
  mothers with a working partner** and **positive for those with a non-working partner**
  (where the household income test does not bite and the woman is effectively the primary
  earner).

## Why it matters for the `other_women` band
The ex-post reduced-form check on the second-earner-disincentive story: even under a
household-income-assessed credit, the *measured* average effect on partnered mothers is a
statistical zero, not a large negative — the negative Brewer et al. (2006) structural
estimate (-0.57pp) and the Eissa-Hoynes U.S. result (~-1pp) sit at the pessimistic edge of
what panel data detect. And the partner-status heterogeneity is exactly the mechanism
split the 80-80 needs: where the household test is not binding (non-working partner), the
in-work credit RAISES partnered women's employment. An individually-assessed subsidy makes
that condition true for every partnered woman by construction. Supports a `other_women`
central value at or above the EITC/CBO-derived default, with the downside risk bounded
near zero-to-small-negative.

## Caveats
- Married mothers (children required for WFTC); childless partnered women out of sample.
- The companion single-mother paper (Francesconi & van der Klaauw 2007, JHR, "The
  Socioeconomic Consequences of 'In-Work' Benefit Reform for British Lone Mothers") finds
  large positive lone-mother effects — cited here only as context; not separately
  verified/cataloged this pass since it duplicates the already-cataloged single-mother
  thread.
- UK, 1999 reform; same external-validity limits as the other WFTC entries.
