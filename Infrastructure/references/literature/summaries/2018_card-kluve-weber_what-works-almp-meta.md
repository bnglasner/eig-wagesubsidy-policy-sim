# Card, Kluve & Weber (2018) — What Works? A Meta-Analysis of Recent ALMP Evaluations

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal of the European Economic Association)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1093/jeea/jvx028
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/jeea/article-abstract/16/3/894/4430618 ; NBER w21431
- **Thread:** B angle 4 (ALMP / employment-program evidence). Companion to Card, Kluve & Weber (2010, EJ).
- **Evidence class:** Meta-analysis of measured/ex-post program evaluations.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Synthesizes 857 program-effect estimates from 207 studies of active labor market
programs across ~50 countries. Effects are typically small/near zero in the short run
but more positive at 2–3 year horizons, especially for human-capital (training) and
private-sector-incentive programs; public-sector employment programs perform worst.
Program effects are larger where unemployment is high. The 2010 EJ predecessor (199
estimates, 97 studies) reached similar conclusions: job-search assistance favorable,
public employment weak, training positive in the medium term.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Sets realistic expectations for how much a labor-market intervention moves employment
and over what horizon. Relevant to the time profile of any induced-employment effect
the 80-80 cost model books: short-run responses are likely small even where long-run
effects exist.
