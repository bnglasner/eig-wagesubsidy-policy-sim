# Meyer & Rosenbaum (2001) — Welfare, the EITC, and the Labor Supply of Single Mothers

- **Tier:** 1 (QJE)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1162/00335530152466313
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/116/3/1063/1899757 ; NBER w7363
- **Thread:** A (program-specific EITC evidence, extensive margin)
- **Evidence class:** Measured/ex-post, structural + quasi-experimental.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Attributes a large share of the 1984–1996 rise in single mothers' employment to the
EITC and other tax changes; the EITC alone explains roughly **60 percent** of the
employment increase over the period. Reports that a **$1,000 increase in AFDC
guarantee at zero earnings reduces employment by ~2.95 pp** (the symmetric force a
positive work incentive would push the other way).

## Conflict note
This positive-employment-effect consensus is exactly what Kleven (2024) later
challenges as fragile to specification and confounded by welfare reform / the 1990s
macroeconomy. Carry both.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Among the strongest measured extensive-margin responses to a work-conditioned
transfer; an upper anchor for the entry response. The AFDC-guarantee semi-elasticity
is a usable input for the income-effect (non-work transfer) side of the model.
