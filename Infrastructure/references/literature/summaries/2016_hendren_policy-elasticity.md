# Hendren (2016) — The Policy Elasticity

- **Tier:** 1 (Tax Policy and the Economy, NBER/Univ. Chicago Press)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1086/685593
- **Source:** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/685593 ; MIT PDF
- **Thread:** B angle 1 (elasticity->revenue feedback) + angle 2 (MVPF method foundation).
- **Evidence class:** Methodology (welfare measurement from causal effects).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Shows that the welfare impact of a policy change can be measured from its causal
behavioral effects without decomposing into Hicksian/Marshallian income and
substitution components; often the single sufficient statistic is the **effect on
government revenue (the fiscal externality)**. Defines the "policy elasticity" — the
behavioral response to the specific policy as implemented — as the welfare-relevant
object. Applies the framework to Moving to Opportunity, where induced earnings/tax
revenue sharply reduce the effective program cost.

## Why it matters for 80-80
The methodological backbone for dynamic cost modeling: the 80-80's net cost should be
computed from its *policy elasticity* (the actual induced earnings/transfer response
to a wage fill), and the key feedback to track is the change in government revenue and
transfer outlays — exactly the dynamic-scoring channel the project wants.
