# Neumark & Grijalva — The Employment Effects of State Hiring Credits

- **Tier:** 1 (NBER working paper w18928; published ILR Review)
- **Type:** paper
- **Source:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w18928 ; sites.socsci.uci.edu/~dneumark/Neumark Grijalva ILRR.pdf
- **Thread:** B angle 4 (hiring-credit / employment-subsidy evaluations).
- **Evidence class:** Measured/ex-post quasi-experimental (state-level policy variation).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Evaluates state hiring credits during/after the Great Recession. Effectiveness is
highly design-dependent: credits targeted at the disadvantaged are generally
ineffective at creating jobs, while non-categorical credits with explicit job-creation
incentives (echoing the 1970s federal New Jobs Tax Credit) can raise employment. Some
recession-era credits did create jobs.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Demonstrates that subsidy *design* (who receives it, how it is conditioned) drives
whether it changes employment — a caution that worker-side vs. employer-side and
targeting choices materially change behavioral and fiscal outcomes.
