# Cahuc, Carcillo & Le Barbanchon (2019) — The Effectiveness of Hiring Credits

- **Tier:** 1 (Review of Economic Studies)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1093/restud/rdy011
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/86/2/593/4951519 ; DOI 10.1093/restud/rdy011 (NBER/IZA working-paper lineage "Do Hiring Credits Work in Recessions? Evidence from France")
- **Thread:** B angle 3 / angle 4 (hiring-credit pass-through and employment effects).
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post) quasi-experimental evaluation of a temporary French hiring credit during the Great Recession.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Evaluates France's temporary 2008–2009 hiring credit for small firms (subsidy to new hires
at/near the minimum wage). Findings:
- The hiring credit **was effective at raising employment when deployed in a deep recession**,
  because in slack labor markets **labor supply is highly elastic and wages are rigid**, so
  the subsidy is **not competed away into higher wages** — it raises hiring rather than pay.
- The same credit would be **much less effective (and more likely to be captured in wages or
  to cause windfalls/displacement) in normal or tight labor markets**, where wages adjust.
- Implication: the employment effect and the pass-through of a hiring/wage subsidy are
  **state-dependent** — slack markets favor employment effects with low wage pass-through;
  tight markets favor wage pass-through and windfall.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Reinforces the Busso et al. (2013) lesson from the tax side: **how much of a wage-linked
subsidy shows up as employment vs. wages depends on labor-market slack and wage rigidity.**
For the 80-80, this argues the incidence/pass-through parameter should be conditioned on
macro slack, and that the favorable (high-worker-benefit, low-capture) case is the
slack-market case.

- **Scope caveat:** Employer-side hiring credit (marginal, temporary, recession-specific),
  France; the 80-80 is a worker-side, permanent, national wage fill. Use for the
  state-dependence lesson, not as a direct elasticity transfer.
