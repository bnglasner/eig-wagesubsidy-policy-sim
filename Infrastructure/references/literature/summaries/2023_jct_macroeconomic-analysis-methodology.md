# Joint Committee on Taxation — Macroeconomic Analysis / Dynamic Revenue Estimating

- **Tier:** 2 (official government economic research, JCT)
- **Type:** technical_note
- **Source:** JCT, "Macroeconomic Analysis at the JCT and the Mechanics of Its Implementation" (JCX-3-15; brookings.edu/.../JCT_analysis.pdf); JCX-34-23; "The JCT Revenue Estimating Process" (Jan 2023)
- **Thread:** B angle 1 (dynamic-scoring methodology — the tax-side scorekeeper).
- **Evidence class:** Modeled/ex-ante methodology.

## Finding (paraphrased)
JCT performs macroeconomic (dynamic) analysis with three models: a Macroeconomic
Equilibrium Growth (MEG) model, an Overlapping Generations (OLG) model, and a DSGE
model. Labor supply responds to marginal and average after-tax wage changes within MEG
and OLG. Conventional JCT revenue estimates already embed taxpayer behavioral responses
(timing, entity shifting, avoidance, labor supply); dynamic analysis additionally
incorporates aggregate labor supply, investment, TFP, saving, incomes, and price/wage
feedback. The House's 2023 dynamic-scoring rule requires point macro-feedback estimates
for major legislation.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Complements the CBO note as the tax-side methodological backbone. If the 80-80 is scored
as a tax/transfer change, JCT-style dynamic methodology defines how induced labor supply
flows into revenue feedback — the recapture channel central to the project's net-cost
question.
