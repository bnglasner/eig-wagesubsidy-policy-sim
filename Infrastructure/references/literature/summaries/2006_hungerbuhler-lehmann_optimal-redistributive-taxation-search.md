# Hungerbühler, Lehmann, Parmentier & Van der Linden (2006) — Optimal Redistributive Taxation in a Search Equilibrium Model

- **Tier:** 1 (Review of Economic Studies)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1467-937x.2006.00394.x
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/73/3/743/1576598 ; REStud 73(3):743-767
- **Thread:** 2 (Nash bargaining and the incidence of a wage/employment subsidy in a matching model).
- **Evidence class:** Modeled (theory). Optimal-tax analysis embedded in a matching model with Nash-bargained wages.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Embeds optimal income taxation/redistribution in a Mortensen-Pissarides matching model
where wages are set by **Nash bargaining over the match surplus**. Because the wage is a
surplus split, a tax or transfer that changes the joint surplus is **not borne by the
party on whom it is statutorily levied** — its economic incidence is divided between worker
and firm according to bargaining power. The paper works through how marginal tax rates and
transfers interact with the bargaining wedge, the participation (employment) margin, and
the search externalities. Key qualitative result: the response of employment and the
distribution of a fiscal intervention's burden/benefit depend on worker bargaining power
beta and the matching-function elasticity (Hosios) — the same parameters that govern
subsidy pass-through.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Thread 2 core)
This is the cleanest published treatment of **why a wage/employment subsidy in a matching
model is split rather than fully captured by the worker**. It micro-founds the incidence
rule the 80-80 simulation uses: a per-worker subsidy s adds to joint surplus
(y + s - r), and is divided beta to the worker and (1 - beta) to the firm, replacing the
aggregate labor-demand-elasticity incidence calculation with a bargaining-based one.
Together with Cahuc-Carcillo-Le Barbanchon (2019, already in catalog) and Rothstein (2010,
already in catalog) it forms the incidence backbone.
