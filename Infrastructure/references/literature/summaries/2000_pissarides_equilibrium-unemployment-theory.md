## Finding (paraphrased)
The standard reference statement of the search-and-matching model, collecting the
Diamond-Mortensen-Pissarides apparatus into the canonical three-equation system:

1. **Matching function** m(u, v) with constant returns, giving the job-finding rate as a
   function of tightness theta = v/u.
2. **Job-creation (free-entry) condition**: the discounted value of a filled job equals
   the vacancy-posting cost; with free entry the vacancy value is zero, pinning down theta.
3. **Wage equation from Nash bargaining**: the wage splits the match surplus,
   w = r + beta*(y - r) in the simplest static-surplus form (with the value of
   non-employment as the worker's threat point and beta the worker's bargaining share).

Chapter 1 develops the baseline model and the surplus/viability logic; later chapters add
job destruction, on-the-job search, and policy (taxes/subsidies). Pissarides is the source
for the convention that the Hosios condition (beta = matching-function elasticity on
unemployment) delivers constrained efficiency, and for typical calibration choices.

## Why it matters for 80-80
This is the citable home base for the exact equations the structural simulation uses: the
surplus-splitting wage rule w = r + beta*(y - r) and the free-entry job-creation condition
that closes the model. Use it as the umbrella reference for the framework alongside the
primary papers (Diamond 1982; Mortensen-Pissarides 1994).
