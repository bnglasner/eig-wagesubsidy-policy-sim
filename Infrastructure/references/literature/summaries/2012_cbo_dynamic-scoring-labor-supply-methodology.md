# CBO — Dynamic Scoring / Labor-Supply Modeling Methodology

- **Tier:** 2 (official government economic research, CBO)
- **Type:** technical_note
- **Source:** "How Does CBO Model the Response of Labor Supply to Changes in Tax and Spending Policies?" (https://www.cbo.gov/publication/43680); "Answers to Questions About Dynamic Analysis" (https://www.cbo.gov/publication/50357)
- **Thread:** B angle 1 (dynamic-scoring methodology).
- **Evidence class:** Modeled/ex-ante methodology (the agency's scoring apparatus).

## Finding (paraphrased)
CBO converts policy-induced changes in after-tax wages into labor-supply responses via
substitution and income elasticities, then feeds the resulting earnings changes into
budget effects (tax revenue, transfer outlays). Central case: earnings-weighted
**total wage elasticity ~0.19** (substitution 0.24, income -0.05). Dynamic scoring
additionally layers macroeconomic feedback (labor supply, investment, output) into
official cost estimates for major legislation; conventional ("static") estimates still
include micro-behavioral responses but hold macro aggregates fixed.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Defines the official methodology a dynamic 80-80 cost model would emulate to estimate
net cost: elasticity -> earnings change -> tax recapture + transfer offsets. The 0.19
central elasticity (with its substitution/income split) is the conventional parameter set.
