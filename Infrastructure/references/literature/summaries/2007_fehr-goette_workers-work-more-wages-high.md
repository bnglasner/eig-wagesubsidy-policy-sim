# Fehr & Goette (2007) — Do Workers Work More if Wages Are High? Evidence from a Randomized Field Experiment

- **Tier:** 1 (American Economic Review)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/aer.97.1.298
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.97.1.298 ; IZA DP 1002 (rev. Oct 2005, PDF read directly this pass)
- **Thread:** intensive-margin evidence for a CLEAN per-hour wage increase without phase-outs (eps_int stress test) — entry-margin reality-anchoring pass, 2026-07-09
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post), randomized field experiment. Zurich bicycle messengers paid pure commission; randomized 25 percent commission increase for four weeks (two treatment groups, staggered).

## Findings (pinned from IZA DP 1002 full text)
- A transitory, fully salient **25 percent increase in the per-delivery wage** raised labor
  supply strongly: **intertemporal (Frisch) elasticity of overall labor supply 1.12-1.25**;
  **wage elasticity of shifts worked 1.34-1.50** (workers supplied ~4 extra shifts per
  4-week period on a base of ~11-12).
- **Effort per shift FELL ~6 percent** (elasticity of effort per shift ~ **-0.24**),
  concentrated entirely among loss-averse workers (reference-dependence, daily income
  targets); non-loss-averse workers show no negative effort response.
- Consistent with Oettinger (1999) stadium vendors (participation elasticity 0.53-0.64).

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Q4: is eps_int = 0.05 defensible for a no-phase-out design?)
The "hours responses are negligible" consensus (Eissa-Liebman 1996; Meyer 2002; both
cataloged) is measured on the **EITC**, whose plateau and phase-out remove or reverse the
marginal-hour incentive over most of its range, and whose annual, tax-time delivery hides
it. This experiment is the cleanest measured counterexample for the opposite design pole:
when the marginal hour's pay rises transparently and without phase-out — structurally the
80-80 case, which raises a $10/hr worker's marginal hour by ~54 percent up to 40 hrs/wk —
workers on the days/shifts margin respond strongly. Calibration caveats going the other
way: (1) this is a **Frisch (transitory)** elasticity, an upper bound for a permanent
subsidy (which carries an offsetting income effect); (2) messengers are self-scheduling —
most employees face hours constraints (Chetty 2012's friction argument, cataloged, central
Hicksian intensive 0.33); (3) the negative effort margin warns that hours and output are
not identical. Verdict this supports: **eps_int = 0.05 imports an EITC design artifact**;
for a salient per-hour no-phase-out subsidy, the defensible central intensive elasticity
for hours-flexible workers sits nearer the Chetty 0.33 Hicksian anchor than 0.05, with
0.05 as a lower bound reflecting hours constraints — not the central case.
