- **Tier:** 1 (Review of Economics and Statistics)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1162/003465303765299891
- **Source:** https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article-abstract/85/2/394/57401 ; NBER w8153 (PDF read directly this pass)
- **Thread:** A / 1990s natural-experiment decomposition (entry-margin reality-anchoring pass, 2026-07-09)
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post). CPS-based panel regressions exploiting cross-state variation in time limits, EITC schedule variation by family size, and cycle variation, 1979-1999 data.

## Findings (pinned from NBER w8153 full text)
Decomposition of the **1993-1999** changes for female-headed families:
- **EITC: explains 34 percent** of the observed increase in employment — "the most
  important single factor" (w8153, p. 29). The main EITC effect: a **$1,000 increase in the
  maximum credit raises employment by 3.6 percentage points**.
- **Unemployment rate (business cycle): accounts for 21 percent** of the employment change
  — second-most important factor.
- **Time limits:** have about **half the proportionate effect on employment as on welfare
  use**; welfare reform as a whole explains **14.3 percent of the 1993-99 decline in welfare
  use**; "other reforms" explain over 6 percent of the employment rise.
- Together, **welfare policy + EITC + business cycle account for 71 percent of the change in
  employment** (but only 24 percent of the change in earnings and 21 percent of income).
- Cross-checks quoted by Grogger: Meyer & Rosenbaum (1999) attribute **37 percent of the
  1992-96 employment increase** to the EITC (the 2001 QJE version's "~60 percent" figure
  refers to the 1984-96 window with all tax changes — the two are consistent, different
  windows/policy bundles).

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Q2: the 1990s macro-scale precedent)
This is the canonical middle-of-road decomposition of the closest historical precedent for
"a large permanent increase in the net return to work for a low-wage population." Read with
Fang & Keane (2004, cataloged): the total single-mother employment-rate rise was on the
order of **+11 pp over roughly a decade (1993-2002)**, of which the EITC — a subsidy worth
several thousand dollars per year, i.e., the same order as the 80-80's per-worker transfer
for its target group — contributed roughly **a third**, with the boom and welfare reform
(time limits + work requirements, i.e., *sticks* absent from the 80-80) contributing the
rest. The full-population, all-instruments, boom-assisted response was ~11pp on a base of
several million single mothers; a wage subsidy alone, without work requirements or time
limits, moved ~3-4pp of it.
