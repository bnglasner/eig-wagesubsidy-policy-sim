# Krueger (2017) — Where Have All the Workers Gone? An Inquiry into the Decline of the U.S. Labor Force Participation Rate

- **Tier:** 1 (Brookings Papers on Economic Activity)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1353/eca.2017.0012
- **Source:** https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/1_krueger.pdf (BPEA Fall 2017 conference version, PDF read directly this pass)
- **Thread:** 3 (target-pool composition and wage-responsiveness: NILF men, NILF women, retirees, youth) — entry-margin reality-anchoring pass, 2026-07-09
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post): CPS/ATUS tabulations plus Krueger's original Princeton Pain Survey (PPS) of NLF prime-age men; county-level opioid-prescription regressions.

## Findings (pinned from the PDF)
**NILF prime-age men (the pool segment the model's high markups describe):**
- "**About half of prime age men who are not in the labor force (NLF) may have a serious
  health condition that is a barrier to work**" (abstract).
- **44 percent** of NLF prime-age men took pain medication the previous day (ATUS-WB;
  **47 percent** in the PPS), vs. ~20 percent of employed men; **nearly two-thirds** of those
  take *prescription* pain medication — i.e., on any given day ~31 percent of NLF prime-age
  men are on likely-opioid medication.
- CPS disability tabulation: at least one functional disability reported for **34 percent**
  of prime-age NLF men (40 percent in some years' tabulations) vs. ~3-6 percent of
  employed/unemployed men.
- **25-35 percent of NLF prime-age men are on SSDI**; ~30 percent of non-recipients had
  previously applied — "around half of all NLF prime age men could have applied to SSDI at
  some point."
- NLF men report notably low emotional well-being and derive little meaning from daily
  activities (adaptation evidence) — but this discontent has not translated into search.
- County-level opioid-prescription growth (3.5x, 1999-2015) could account for **~0.6pp of
  the male LFP decline, i.e., ~20 percent of the observed decline in that period** (the
  widely quoted "43 percent" figure is from an earlier draft/press coverage; the version
  read here states 20 percent — treat 20 percent as the citable number for this vintage).

**NILF women:** in 2015, **60 percent** of NLF prime-age women cite home responsibilities
as the main reason for not working (down from 77 percent in 1991); NLF women's subjective
well-being is close to employed women's — "not... discontent with their lives or daily
routines, and therefore eager to return to work."

**Retirees:** the 16+ retirement rate rose from 15.4 to 17.6 percent 2007-2017; this
**2.2pp rise almost matches the 2.8pp drop in LFP** — retirements "tend to be permanent
exits."

**Youth:** rising school enrollment "largely offset" declining young-worker participation
since the 1990s.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Q3: sizing the realistic entry-risk set)
This is the most direct evidence that the model's non-employed pool is NOT a reserve army
priced just out of the market: roughly half of the core prime-age male NILF segment faces
serious health barriers, a quarter-to-a-third are on SSDI (with benefit-loss disincentives
a wage subsidy does not remove), most NILF prime-age women cite caregiving and report
little revealed desire to return, retirees are permanent exits, and 16-24s are largely in
school. High calibrated reservation markups for these groups are consistent with this
evidence — what looks like "15.6x the net gain" is substantially health, caregiving,
benefit-interaction, and permanent-exit structure, not free-floating choosiness a
wage fill can buy off.
