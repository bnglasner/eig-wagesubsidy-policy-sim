# Card & Hyslop (2005) — Estimating the Effects of a Time-Limited Earnings Subsidy for Welfare-Leavers

- **Tier:** 1 (Econometrica)
- **Type:** paper
- **Source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00637.x ; NBER w10647 (working-paper version)
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00637.x
- **Thread:** B angle 4 (direct earnings-supplement / wage-fill program evaluation — the closest experimental analog to the 80-80).
- **Evidence class:** Measured/ex-post — randomized social experiment (Canadian Self-Sufficiency Project, SSP).

## Finding (paraphrased)
The SSP randomly offered single-parent welfare recipients a generous earnings supplement
(roughly doubling earnings, akin to filling the gap up to a target) conditional on
full-time work (30+ hours/week), available for three years but only to those who began
full-time work within 12 months of random assignment. The authors separate two
behavioral channels: an "establishment" incentive (find full-time work within the
12-month window) and an "entitlement" incentive (choose work over welfare once eligible).
The supplement raised full-time employment substantially during the eligibility window,
but the effect faded once payments ended — i.e., a largely transitory employment response
to a time-limited work-conditioned subsidy.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (directly narrows the #1 gap)
This is the cleanest measured evidence on an *earnings supplement conditioned on work* —
structurally the nearest experimental cousin to a wage-fill design. Two lessons port to
the 80-80: (1) the extensive-margin (entry) response to a work-conditioned supplement is
real and sizeable, distinguishing it from the unconditional NIT result; (2) the magnitude
and persistence of the response depend on whether the subsidy is permanent vs.
time-limited — the 80-80, as a permanent fill, would not share SSP's fade-out, but SSP
provides an upper-bound-style benchmark on entry response and a caution about modeling
transitory vs. steady-state behavior.

## Caveats
- Canadian welfare-leaver population; external validity to the broad U.S. 80-80-eligible
  workforce is limited (single parents on welfare, not all low-wage workers).
- Time-limited design (3-year window) differs from the permanent 80-80; the fade-out is a
  feature of SSP's sunset, not necessarily of a permanent fill.

### (a) Full-time employment effect — pinned (recipient sample)

All numbers below are for the **recipient demonstration** (single parents on IA in at
least 11 of the prior 12 months; 2,858 program / 2,826 control, randomly assigned
Nov 1992–Feb 1995 in BC and NB; w10647 Table 1). Impact-table sample = 4,852
respondents to the 54-month survey (SRDC Table ES.1).

- **Yearly-average monthly full-time employment impacts (SRDC 2002, Table ES.1):**
  Year 1 **+6.4pp\*\*\*** (18.0 vs 11.6); Year 2 **+12.6pp\*\*\*** (28.5 vs 16.0 — full-time
  employment more than *doubled* relative to control); Year 3 +9.3pp\*\*\*; Year 4
  +6.1pp\*\*\*; Year 5 Q1 +3.3pp\*\*\*; Year 5 Q2 +1.5pp (n.s.). "Full-time" = 30+ hours in
  at least one week of the month.
- **Peak monthly full-time employment impact: ~15pp at the beginning of Year 2**
  (SRDC 2002, Figure 3.1 and accompanying text: "the impact on monthly full-time
  employment peaked at about 15 percentage points at the beginning of Year 2 and fell
  to about 2 to 3 percentage points by the beginning of Year 5").
- **Any-employment (not full-time) impacts** are smaller: Year 2 yearly average
  **+10.4pp\*\*\*** (40.6 vs 30.1; SRDC Table ES.1); monthly profile peaks at ~14pp in
  month 13, declines to ~6pp by month 36, and reaches 0 by month 53 (w10647, Figure 3a
  and p. 11 text).
- Companion welfare result (w10647, Figure 1/p. 9): IA-participation impact peaked at
  **−14pp** around month 15, −7pp by month 36.

### (b) Earnings gain and supplement generosity — pinned

- **Average monthly earnings impact (SRDC Table ES.1):** Year 1 +$47\*\*\*; **Year 2
  +$101\*\*\*** ($370 program vs $269 control — a ~38 percent gain relative to control at
  peak); Year 3 +$70\*\*\*; Year 4 +$52\*\*; Year 5 not significant. Per supplement
  *taker* (~36 percent of the program group took up), SSP increased full-time work
  experience by nearly a year and **earnings by more than $9,000** cumulatively, and
  combined income (earnings + IA + supplement) by about $17,600 (SRDC, ES-13).
- **Supplement formula (w10647 Table 1; SRDC "Key Features" box):** supplement = **half
  the difference between actual earnings and an earnings benchmark** ($2,500/month in NB,
  $3,083/month in BC in 1993 — i.e., $30,000 and $37,000 annualized in the first
  operating year), conditional on 30+ hours/week at at least the minimum wage, payable
  for 36 months, forfeited if not initiated within 12 months of random assignment.
- **"Roughly doubling earnings" — verified as approximately correct for low-wage
  full-time workers, pre-tax.** The SRDC report states verbatim that "the supplement
  roughly doubled the earnings of many low-wage workers (before taxes and work-related
  expenses)." The w10647 worked example (p. 5): a NB single mother working full time at
  the minimum wage grossed $867/month and would receive an $817/month supplement —
  i.e., the supplement was ≈94 percent of gross earnings at the NB minimum wage.
  **Qualifier that must travel with the claim:** SSP payments were taxable and affected
  subsidized daycare, so the payoff *net* of taxes and transfers was about 30 percent
  smaller (w10647 p. 5, citing Lin et al. 1998 Table G.1).

### Recipient vs. applicant sample — distinction now explicit

- Card & Hyslop (2005) and all numbers above concern the **recipient** demonstration
  (long-term welfare recipients).
- The separate **applicant** study (new IA applicants, who had to remain on IA 12 months
  to qualify) is reported in Ford, Gyarmati, Foley, Tattrie & Jimenez, *Can Work
  Incentives Pay for Themselves? Final Report on the Self-Sufficiency Project for
  Welfare Applicants*, SRDC, 2006 (srdc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/SSP72.pdf; also
  retrieved and read this pass). Headline applicant results: full-time employment impact
  peaked at **+11.7pp in Year 3** (with −10.3pp IA receipt), impacts **persisted five
  years** (including a year in which no one received the supplement), and program group
  members earned on average **$7,859 more** over the first 71 months. The applicant
  sample thus shows *more persistence* than the recipient sample's fade-out — the
  fade-out lesson in this summary is specific to the recipient study Card & Hyslop
  analyze. (Card & Hyslop's separate applicant-sample paper is not cataloged here.)

### Status of prior characterizations

- "Raised full-time employment substantially during the eligibility window, fading once
  payments ended" — **confirmed** (peak ~15pp monthly / +12.6pp Year-2 average → ~2-3pp
  by start of Year 5, n.s. by Year 5 Q2).
- "Roughly doubling earnings" — **confirmed with qualifiers** (pre-tax, at
  minimum-wage-level earnings; ~30 percent smaller net of taxes/transfers).
