# Chetty, Guren, Manoli & Weber (2011) — Micro vs. Macro Labor Supply Elasticities

- **Tier:** 1 (AER Papers & Proceedings)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/aer.101.3.471
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.101.3.471 ; rajchetty.com PDF
- **Thread:** A (elasticity consensus anchor)
- **Evidence class:** Review/meta-synthesis of measured estimates.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Reconciles the gap between large macro-calibrated elasticities and small micro
estimates. Micro estimates are consistent with macro **steady-state (Hicksian)**
elasticities on both margins; the persistent puzzle is the **Frisch** (intertemporal
substitution) extensive-margin elasticity, where micro estimates are roughly an
order of magnitude below the values macro business-cycle models require. Companion
NBER Macro Annual meta-analysis (w16729) aggregates extensive-margin estimates.

## Reported magnitudes (as stated)
- Intensive-margin Hicksian elasticity central estimate ~0.33; extensive ~0.26
  (consistent with Chetty 2012). Frisch extensive elasticity micro ~0.25 vs. macro ~3.

## Why it matters for 80-80
Tells the cost model which elasticity concept to use: a permanent wage-fill subsidy
is a steady-state (Hicksian/Marshallian) change, so the smaller micro-consistent
steady-state elasticities — not the large macro Frisch values — are the right inputs.
