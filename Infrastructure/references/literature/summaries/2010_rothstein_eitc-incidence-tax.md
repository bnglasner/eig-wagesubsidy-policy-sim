# Rothstein (2010) — Is the EITC as Good as an NIT? Conditional Cash Transfers and Tax Incidence

- **Tier:** 1 (AEJ: Economic Policy)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/pol.2.1.177
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.2.1.177 ; NBER w14966
- **Thread:** B angle 3 (incidence & wage pass-through) — the load-bearing pass-through paper.
- **Evidence class:** Modeled incidence calibrated on measured elasticities (ex-ante incidence simulation).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Because the EITC raises low-skill labor supply, it depresses the market wage, so part
of the transfer is captured by employers. Under preferred parameters, low-skill single
mothers **keep only ~$0.70 of each EITC dollar**; employers capture ~$0.72 ($0.30 from
recipients plus $0.43 from EITC-ineligible low-skill workers whose wages also fall).
By contrast, $1 of NIT spending raises after-tax incomes by ~$1.39 (no work-incentive
wage depression). EITC: $1 -> ~$0.73 of after-tax income.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (critical)
The 80-80 is a direct-to-worker wage fill, but its design interacts with the labor
market: if it induces entry/hours, equilibrium wages may fall and employers may capture
part of the subsidy, changing both effective worker benefit and distribution. A
credible dynamic 80-80 cost model needs an incidence/pass-through parameter — Rothstein
is the anchor for that parameter and for whether the gross transfer overstates net
worker gain.
