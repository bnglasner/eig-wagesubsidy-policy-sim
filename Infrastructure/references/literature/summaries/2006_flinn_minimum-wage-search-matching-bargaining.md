# Flinn (2006) — Minimum Wage Effects on Labor Market Outcomes under Search, Matching, and Endogenous Contact Rates

- **Tier:** 1 (Econometrica)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00693.x
- **Source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00693.x ; Econometrica 74(4):1013-1062
- **Thread:** 5 (wage subsidies / minimum wage in search-matching with bargaining).
- **Evidence class:** Modeled + measured (structural search model estimated on US data).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Builds and **structurally estimates** a search-matching model with **Nash-bargained wages
and endogenous contact rates**, then studies how a **binding minimum wage** interacts with
bargaining. Key mechanics: when the minimum wage binds, it truncates the lower part of the
bargained-wage distribution; matches whose bargained wage would fall below the minimum
either are not formed (the surplus net of the wage floor turns negative) or pay the
floor. Because contact rates respond endogenously, the employment effect of the minimum
wage is not the textbook competitive prediction and can even be non-negative over a range
(the model recovers conditions under which a minimum wage raises welfare). Flinn estimates
worker bargaining power as part of the structural exercise.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Thread 5 core)
The 80-80's induced entrants land near the minimum wage, so the interaction of the wage
floor with surplus-splitting bargaining is first-order. Flinn is the canonical treatment
showing **how a binding minimum wage reshapes the bargained-wage distribution and the
viability (surplus) margin** in exactly the matching-with-bargaining framework the 80-80
uses. It supplies: (a) the floor-truncation logic for the wage equation when w would
otherwise be below the minimum, and (b) an independent **structural estimate of worker
bargaining power beta** to cross-check the calibrated value.
