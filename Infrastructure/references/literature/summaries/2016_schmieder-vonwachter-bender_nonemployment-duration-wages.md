# Schmieder, von Wachter & Bender (2016) — The Effect of Unemployment Benefits and Nonemployment Durations on Wages

- **Tier:** 1 (American Economic Review)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1257/aer.20141566
- **Source:** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20141566 ; NBER w19772 (PDF read directly this pass)
- **Thread:** 5 (wage-scale sanity: what wages do entrants from nonemployment actually command?) — entry-margin reality-anchoring pass, 2026-07-09
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post), quasi-experimental. German administrative day-level records 1975-2008; sharp age discontinuities in potential UI duration; RD/IV.

## Findings (pinned from NBER w19772)
- Causal IV estimate: **each additional month of nonemployment reduces reemployment wage
  offers by 0.8 percent** ("a statistically significant and substantial reduction in wage
  offers of 0.8%" per month; abstract states a causal effect of nonemployment duration on
  wages of 0.8% per month).
- Reduced form: post-unemployment wages ~0.8 percent lower at the UI-extension
  discontinuity; UI extensions lower reemployment wages mainly *through* longer
  nonemployment, not through reservation-wage choosiness — i.e., **the wage offer
  distribution itself deteriorates with time out of work**.
- Implication over a spell: a worker nonemployed for 24 months faces offers roughly
  **~17-18 percent below** an otherwise-identical continuously-attached worker; the effect
  compounds with duration and is a *decline in offered wages*, not a composition artifact.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (Q5: does the MPL imputation overstate the pool's wages?)
The model imputes non-workers' potential wages from the structure of ALL employed earners
(median $26.00, 44 percent salaried), while the policy target ($16.80 fill toward a $21.00
paid-hourly median) prices hourly jobs. This paper is the sharpest causal evidence that
**potential wages of the long-nonemployed sit well below observationally-equivalent
incumbents' wages**: skills/signals decay at ~0.8 percent per month of nonemployment. Much
of the 59M pool (especially the 33.8M NILF-other and 9.7M disabled) has been out for years,
implying offer wages 10-30+ percent below an employed-earner imputation *before* any
selection-on-unobservables correction. Two model implications: (1) the imputed potential
wages for the long-nonemployed are biased UP, so more of the pool falls below the $16.80
target than the imputation implies — which *raises* the subsidy-eligible share but *lowers*
the private wage on which the fill stacks; (2) pairs with Krueger-Mueller (2016, cataloged):
accepted reemployment wages average ~0.90 of the prior wage even for the recently
unemployed — entrants take entry-priced jobs, not incumbent-priced jobs.
