# Popp (2023) — How Elastic Is Labor Demand? A Meta-Analysis for the German Labor Market

- **Tier:** 1 (Journal for Labour Market Research; open access)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12651-023-00337-8
- **Source:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12651-023-00337-8 ; IAB project page
- **Thread:** B angle 3 (incidence & wage pass-through) — external (German) cross-check on the own-wage labor-demand elasticity magnitude and its skill gradient.
- **Evidence class:** Meta-analysis of measured (ex-post) estimates.

## Finding (paraphrased)
Meta-analysis of **705 own-wage labor-demand elasticity estimates from 105 studies** on
the German labor market. Average own-wage elasticity of labor demand ≈ **−0.43**. Labor
demand is **more elastic (more negative) for low-skilled (and high-skilled) workers, in
the long run, and for internationally operating firms.** Empirical designs that more
credibly address endogeneity deliver more negative elasticities; the analysis does not
find systematic differences by region or by margin of adjustment.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (incidence module)
A non-US cross-check on the demand-elasticity magnitude and skill gradient that anchor
the incidence module. Popp's −0.43 sits modestly above (in absolute value) the
US/international ~−0.3 consensus (Hamermesh 1993; Lichter et al. 2015), and reinforces
the central qualitative input for the 80-80: **demand is more elastic for low-skilled
workers**, so a low-wage fill that shifts low-skill supply outward should be expected to
push down equilibrium low-skill wages more than the all-worker average implies. Use as a
sensitivity/high-magnitude reference point, not the US central case (German labor market;
different institutions).
