- **Evidence class:** Modeled (theory). Equilibrium search model; no estimation.

## Finding (paraphrased)
One of the three founding "DMP" papers. A worker and firm who meet form a match that
yields a joint surplus; because each meeting is a bilateral monopoly (search frictions
make the next partner costly to find), the wage is not pinned down by Walrasian
market-clearing but by **splitting the match surplus** between the two parties. Diamond
shows that the privately bargained wage generically does **not** coincide with the
socially efficient one (search externalities), motivating the later Hosios (1990)
efficiency condition. The core object is the bilateral surplus and how it is divided.

## Why it matters for 80-80
This is the theoretical origin of the surplus-splitting wage rule the 80-80 incidence
module uses: w = r + beta*(y - r). It establishes that with search frictions the wage is
a division of surplus (worker outside option r plus a share beta of the match rent
y - r), which is precisely the channel through which a per-worker subsidy s is split
between worker and firm rather than fully passed to the worker.
