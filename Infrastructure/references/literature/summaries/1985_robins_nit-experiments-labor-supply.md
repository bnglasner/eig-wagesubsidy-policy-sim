# NIT Income-Maintenance Experiments — Robins (1985), Burtless, Gary (Burtless & Hausman 1978)

- **Tier:** 1 (JHR / JPE / conference volume)
- **Type:** paper
- **Source:** Robins (1985) JHR survey (DOI 10.2307/145685); Burtless & Hausman (1978) JPE "Gary NIT" (DOI 10.1086/260730); IRP SIME/DIME archive (https://www.irp.wisc.edu/research/nit/)
- **Thread:** A (NIT experimental evidence — the measured benchmark for unconditional transfers).
- **Evidence class:** Measured/ex-post randomized experiments (the four U.S. income-maintenance experiments: New Jersey, Rural, Gary, SIME/DIME).

## Finding (paraphrased)
The four NIT experiments produced moderate-to-large proportional reductions in work
effort; responses were larger for women than men. Robins (1985) surveys single-parent
results: all estimated impacts negative in sign, statistically significant mainly for
Black households in Seattle/Denver and Hispanic households in Denver. Burtless &
Hausman (1978) evaluate the Gary experiment's labor-supply effect with nonlinear
budget-set methods.

## Why it matters for 80-80
The NIT experiments are the canonical evidence that an *unconditional* guarantee
reduces labor supply (income effect). The 80-80 is *conditional on working* (a wage
fill, not a guarantee), so this literature defines the contrast: the 80-80 should
avoid the NIT's negative-participation force but still carries an income effect on
hours among inframarginal workers — a parameter the model needs.
