# Saez, Matsaganis & Tsakloglou (2012) — Earnings Determination and Taxes: Evidence from a Cohort-Based Payroll Tax Reform in Greece

- **Tier:** 1 (Quarterly Journal of Economics)
- **Type:** paper
- **DOI:** 10.1093/qje/qjr052
- **Source:** https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/127/1/493/1924221 ; DOI 10.1093/qje/qjr052
- **Thread:** B angle 3 (incidence & wage pass-through) — direct measured evidence on how a payroll-tax wedge splits between worker earnings and employer cost.
- **Evidence class:** Measured (ex-post) quasi-experimental incidence study, exploiting a sharp cohort cutoff (workers entering after a date faced higher payroll-tax rates).

## Finding (paraphrased)
Greece set payroll-tax rates by entry cohort: workers who started employment after a
threshold date faced substantially higher employer + employee payroll-tax rates than
otherwise-identical workers hired just before. Comparing adjacent cohorts:
- **Employer-side payroll taxes are borne by the employer (not shifted to workers) in the
  short/medium run** — there is **no pass-through of the employer payroll tax onto
  posted/gross wages** across cohorts. The earnings of high-tax and low-tax cohorts are
  essentially the same; the firm absorbs the employer tax.
- **Employee-side payroll taxes are borne by employees.** Statutory incidence largely
  sticks: each side effectively bears the tax legally assigned to it.
- This contradicts the textbook full-shifting/elastic-supply benchmark and points to
  **wage rigidities / a salient statutory-incidence norm** preventing the wedge from being
  competed into wages.

## Why it matters for 80-80 (incidence benchmark)
A clean measured benchmark for the pass-through question from the tax side. It shows that
**statutory incidence can stick** — taxes (and by symmetry, subsidies) do not automatically
get shifted to the other side of the market via wage adjustment, especially where wages are
rigid. For the 80-80, this is the counter-pole to Rothstein-style full wage adjustment: if
employer-side wedges do not pass through to wages, a worker-side subsidy may likewise be
retained by workers rather than competed away. Use to bound the pass-through parameter
toward "low employer capture" in rigid-wage settings.

- **Scope caveat:** Greek institutional setting and a payroll-tax (not a wage-subsidy)
  reform; the symmetry between tax and subsidy incidence is a theoretical extension, not a
  direct measurement of a subsidy.
