Executive Summary

Overall Methodological Risk: HIGH
This is a calibrated structural simulation (Heckman-imputed potential wages, an elasticity-calibrated entry lottery, and a Nash-bargaining matching stage), not a causal-inference paper, so the standard DiD/RD/IV checklists do not apply; the review adapts the Descriptive, Sample & Data, Standard-Error, functional-form, and Dataset-Variable rules and otherwise uses econometric judgment. Ten concerns are reported. The two HIGH items concern the headline center (0.83M entrants) and the shape of the uncertainty band. On Q1 the review confirms from code that all three named conservatisms push entry down (no penalty: pen10/pen20 raise entry to 1.21M/1.70M; λ=0.75 vs full-residual λ=1.00 raises entry to 1.12M; residual σ is estimated on accepted, below-truncated paid-hourly wages with no Flinn correction), and that the central applies zero of a non-employment penalty the methodology doc itself calls the “dominant MPL uncertainty axis.” On that MPL/dispersion side the center is biased low. But this is not a one-sided story: the behavioral side carries offsetting upward permissiveness (no fixed cost of work, 100% take-up, frictionless matching at find-rate 1, NI(0) omitting UI/SSDI), so the net direction is genuinely ambiguous — the specific, hardest-to-defend choice is penalty = 0 at the central while the doc calls that axis dominant. On Q2, the nine variants are all one-at-a-time and the axes interact multiplicatively through reachability, so the published envelope understates the joint upper tail. On Q3 the men's-cell “double count” is verified absent (the mean-1 normalization leaves the cell total invariant; realized≈target confirms eps alone sets the count). On Q4 the calibration converges and the min(0.90,·) target cap is structurally non-binding given ceiling_ext=1.5 (per-person response is capped at 0.5). On Q5 the entrant-hours rank-rank map (mean 768/yr) is an untested cost driver, and the incumbent hours margin runs on gross-g while the entry gate runs on net g_net — an internal inconsistency that inflates the hours channel. Recommend addressing MR-001 and MR-002 (and the presentation of the band) before publication.

Research Design Assessment

Inferred Research Design

Identification Strategy: Calibrated structural microsimulation: Heckman two-step MPL imputation → elasticity-calibrated entry lottery (λ-bisection on a net, saturating criterion) → Mortensen-Pissarides / Nash-bargaining matching stage. Not a reduced-form causal design.
Treatment / Policy: 80-80 per-hour wage-fill subsidy to a $16.80 target; entry = non-employed persons made viable on the net criterion.
Outcome Variable(s): Induced entrants (headline 0.83M), entrant hours/FTE, gross & net fiscal cost, incumbent hours margin.
Unit of Analysis: Person (non-employed 16-64), CPS ORG rosters; 181,960 pool rows, weighted population 59.28M.
Standard Errors: None propagated. Published band = eps_ext scenario uncertainty only (disclosed, ST-9); no sampling/bootstrap error.
Weights: WTFINL (basic-monthly person weight; divided by n_months for stock). Heckman probit/OLS run UNWEIGHTED.
Data Source: CPS ORG raw partitions (last 2 years), household links via RELATE rosters (01i); PolicyEngine schedules for NI().
Key Packages: numpy, pandas, statsmodels (Probit/OLS), scipy.stats.norm.

MR-001
Q1 — Headline center (0.83M) stacks three verified downward conservatisms; penalty = 0 is the weakest link
01h_nonemployed_pool.py / 00_config.py · Causal Inference
HIGH
Relevant Code
# 01h: three levers, all defaulting to the conservative side at the headline
_MPL_PENALTY = float(os.environ.get("EIG_MPL_PENALTY", "0"))          # (a) NO duration penalty
_OFFER_LAMBDA = float(os.environ.get("EIG_MPL_LAMBDA",
    str(cfg["matching"]["offer_dispersion"]["lambda_central"])))     # (b) 0.75, below full-residual 1.00
# (c) sigma_g built from ols.resid on ACCEPTED paid-hourly wages (truncated below); no Flinn correction
sd_grp = resid_df.groupby(["ef","ag"])["r"].agg(["std","size"])       # residual SD of accepted wages
spread = np.exp(ls * norm.ppf(u_wage) - 0.5*ls**2)                    # ls = 0.75 * sigma_g
Why This Is a Concern
The direction of each named conservatism is confirmed from the produced outputs, and all three lower entry:
• (a) No non-employment penalty. EIG_MPL_PENALTY defaults to 0. Adding the evidence-anchored penalty raises entry: mpl_imputation_band.parquet shows pen10 → 1.21M and pen20 → 1.70M vs the 0.83M headline. The methodology doc (revision 3) itself calls this axis “the dominant MPL uncertainty axis,” anchored to Schmieder-von Wachter-Bender (2016, ~0.8%/month offer decay) and Krueger-Mueller (2016, accepted ~0.90× prior) — yet the headline applies none of it.
• (b) λ=0.75 offer dispersion. Full-residual λ=1.00 gives 1.12M vs 0.83M; λ=0.50 gives 0.62M. Trimming to 0.75 removes low-tail mass and lowers reachability (below-target 41.4% at λ=0.75 vs 45.9% at λ=1.00).
• (c) Truncated residual σ. The dispersion SD is the OLS residual SD of accepted paid-hourly wages, which are truncated from below (rejected/low offers and the min-wage floor are unobserved). No Flinn (2006) / Heckman correction reinflates the missing low tail, so the offer σ used to spread MPL is itself understated → too little below-target mass → fewer entrants.

Verdict (balanced). On the MPL/dispersion side the center is biased low, and the single hardest choice to defend is penalty = 0 while the doc calls the penalty axis dominant. But the build is not one-sided: the behavioral/frictions side is upward-permissive and disclosed (no fixed cost of work M6; 100% take-up; frictionless matching at find-rate 1, E6; NI(0) omits UI/SSDI, ST-3). These offset the MPL conservatism to an unknown degree, so a categorical “the true center is 1.46M” is not supported by the evidence either — 1.46M is the status-mixture skew variant, which layers a large left-skew penalty and is a legitimate upper scenario, not a demonstrated center. The defensible reading is that 0.83M ≈ 3.4pp of the 24.5M reachable pool sits at/below the EITC-alone precedent (~3.5-4pp), i.e. on the conservative side, and that the penalty=0 convention drives most of that.
What to Check / Recommendation
  • Adopt a modest central non-employment penalty (e.g. 5-10%, weighted to labor-market-distant statuses) or present a penalty-inclusive co-central alongside the penalty-0 figure, so the headline does not silently sit at the floor of an axis the doc calls dominant.
  • Reconcile the doc's own framing: the banner still describes 0.46M as “central” in places and 0.83M elsewhere — pin one headline and state which levers define it.
  • Document the offsetting upward biases (M6 fixed cost, take-up, matching friction, NI(0) transfers) next to the downward MPL conservatisms so readers can see the net direction is uncertain, not one-signed.
  • Do not describe the band as symmetric model uncertainty: state explicitly that the penalty=0 / λ=0.75 / truncated-σ center is a lower-leaning anchor.
MR-002
Q2 — Nine one-at-a-time variants understate the joint envelope because the axes interact multiplicatively
02f_mpl_imputation_band.py / 02d_matching_simulation.py · Causal Inference
HIGH
Relevant Code
# 02f: every variant perturbs ONE env var from the conditional/central pool
_VARIANTS = { "conditional":..., "mills0":..., "plain":...,          # imputation axis
              "pen10":..., "pen20":...,                               # penalty axis
              "lam050":..., "lam100":...,                             # dispersion axis
              "skewstat":..., "skewstat_hi":... }                     # skew axis
# 02d entry_margin_band: eps band + take-up + spouse + estock, each varied alone
Why This Is a Concern
The axes are not separable. A penalty lowers MPL, which simultaneously raises reachability (pen20: below-target 41.4% → 63.6%), raises each reachable person's g_net, and shifts entrant composition; the λ-bisection then re-solves per cell, and dispersion interacts with the quantile-matched entry hours (both keyed to the MPL rank). Because entry scales roughly with reachability × response, the effect of combining two upward levers is super-additive, not the max of the two marginals. The one-at-a-time outputs top out at pen20 = 1.70M and skewstat_hi = 1.99M, but a joint corner (e.g. a 10-20% penalty and full-residual λ=1.00 and the eps upper edge) would plausibly exceed 2.5-3M — outside the published spread. Presenting the variants as the model-uncertainty range therefore understates the true upper tail, in the same policy-relevant direction as MR-001. A caveat in the other direction: the saturation ceiling (MR-008) and the g_net cap compress high-g_net responses, so combinations are partly sub-additive at the extreme — which is exactly why the interaction needs to be computed rather than assumed.
What to Check / Recommendation
  • Add a small set of joint corner scenarios (the two or three most-interacting axes crossed, e.g. penalty × dispersion × eps-edge) rather than only one-at-a-time rows; report the joint min/max as the true envelope.
  • Do not jump to a full probabilistic Monte Carlo: the axes are transported priors with no jointly-estimated distribution, so a full joint sampler would manufacture false precision. A 2×2×2 or 3×3 grid is the proportionate treatment for a Tier-1 public simulation.
  • State in the draft that the widest published number is a one-at-a-time bound, and that the joint upper is higher; give at least one computed joint-corner figure so readers are not misled by “1.99M” as a ceiling.

MR-003
Q3b — Band-edge anchors were converted at net stimulus ~0.7 but applied at realized median g_net 0.13-0.19
00_config.py (eps_ext_band provenance) / 01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
# 00_config.py — single_mothers upper 0.65:
#   "SSP-anchored (implied eps~0.49 from +10.4pp on 30.1% base over net stimulus ~0.7 ...)"
# Diagnostics (realized): median_g_net = sm 0.1298 / ow 0.183 / men 0.1457   # ~0.13-0.19, not 0.7
target = min(0.90, mean(response_multiplier(eps, gr, 1.5) - 1))   # gr = realized g_net
Why This Is a Concern
The anchor eps was derived by dividing a program's finite employment response (SSP: +10.4pp on a 30.1% base ≈ 0.346) by that program's net stimulus (asserted ~0.7), then applied by multiplying by the 80-80's realized net stimulus (~0.13-0.19). That is the correct elasticity-transport arithmetic — so the anchor is not mechanically “broken” by the dispersion rebase. Two real issues remain. First, the “0.7” net-stimulus basis is an unverified config-comment assertion; if SSP's stimulus were measured on a less-clawed-back basis than the 80-80's taxable/countable net g_net, the two stimuli are on different scales and the ratio mixes them. Second, eps is used as a local (tangent) slope at g→0, but it was backed out as a secant slope over [0, 0.7]; for a concave/saturating response the secant understates the tangent, so applying the divided-down eps at the small realized g (~0.13) tends to understate single-mother response — reinforcing, not offsetting, the MR-001 direction. This deserves disclosure because the SSP edge is the single most consequential upper anchor.
What to Check
  • Document and, if possible, verify the ~0.7 net-stimulus basis used to back out each edge eps from its source program; confirm it is measured the same way as the model's net g_net.
  • State the constant-elasticity-across-stimulus-scale assumption explicitly (eps identified at g≈0.7, applied at g≈0.13-0.19).
  • Note the secant-vs-tangent direction: dividing a large-stimulus response by its stimulus understates the marginal slope under saturation, so the derived edges are, if anything, conservative.

MR-004
Q5b — Incumbent hours margin uses GROSS g while the entry gate uses NET g_net (internal inconsistency)
02d_matching_simulation.py (incumbent_hours_margin) · Regression Specification
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
# 02d incumbent_hours_margin: GROSS proportional wage gain
g = np.divide(s_hr, w0, out=np.zeros_like(s_hr), where=w0 > 0)     # gross g
mult = _b.response_multiplier(float(eps), g, sat_int)             # eps_int on gross g
# vs 01h entry gate, deliberately on NET return-to-work:
g_net = (ni_package - ni_base) / net_gain_base                    # net g_net (median 0.13-0.19)
Why This Is a Concern
The entry margin was deliberately moved to a NET basis (ST-2) precisely because the taxable/countable subsidy design claws back much of the gross gain — median net g_net (0.13-0.19) is roughly half the gross g for men/other women and far less for single mothers deep in phase-out. The incumbent intensive margin, however, applies eps_int to the gross proportional wage gain (docstring: “gross-g stimulus, 02b convention”), i.e. a stimulus 3-7× larger than the net one used for entry. The marginal hour's true incentive is the gross hourly gain × (1 − EMTR); using gross assumes EMTR≈0, which the entry side already demonstrated is false. This overstates the hours response, and the channel is material — central added FTE is 0.25M (added net cost $1.9B), close to the 0.32M entry FTE. Two headline labor-supply channels should not be calibrated on different stimulus definitions.
What to Check
  • Move the incumbent hours margin to the same net marginal-hour basis as the entry gate (net return to the extra hours after EMTR), or report both gross- and net-basis rows and flag which is the headline.
  • For part-timers below all phase-out regions gross≈net, so the correction may be small for some cells — compute it rather than assume it.
  • Ensure the 02b benchmark scenario table (which legitimately keeps gross-g for its labeled EITC/CBO reduced form) is not conflated with the structural incumbent margin in the draft.
MR-005
Q5a — Entrant-hours rank-rank map (mean 768/yr) is an untested cost driver
01h_nonemployed_pool.py (_entry_hours) · Regression Specification
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
ranks = pd.Series(mpl[pidx]).rank(method="average", pct=True).to_numpy()
hours[pidx] = np.quantile(ih, np.clip(ranks, 0.0, 1.0))   # MPL pctile -> incumbent-hours pctile, within cell
Why This Is a Concern
Each entrant is assigned the annual hours of the same within-cell percentile of the incumbent hours distribution, ranked by imputed MPL. This bundles two untested assumptions: (i) an entrant's MPL rank equals their hours rank (a perfect positive productivity-hours rank correlation transported from incumbents), and (ii) entrants' hours distribution equals incumbents'. The result (mean 768/yr ≈ 15 hrs/wk, verified in entry_margin_band.parquet) is very part-time, and because gross cost is subsidy × hours, this low figure directly produces the low marginal cost per entrant that makes the policy look cheap (entry FTE 0.32M on 0.83M entrants). It has never been given a sensitivity band. The concern cuts both ways: the status lottery now makes 35% of entrants prior-unemployed job-seekers, who plausibly target full-time hours — mapping them onto an incumbent distribution full of part-timers may understate their hours and thus cost; conversely student-age entrants may work even less. Also, MPL rank is now partly a hash-quantile dispersion draw, so the hours assignment inherits that noise.
What to Check
  • Add a sensitivity band on entrant hours: (a) flat full-time (2000h) upper bound; (b) cell-mean incumbent hours regardless of rank; (c) status-conditioned hours (prior-unemployed → higher); (d) rank correlation broken (random within-cell assignment).
  • Report cost-per-entrant and net cost under each — this is the driver of the “low marginal cost” result.
  • Justify or relax the assumption that entrants' hours mirror incumbents' distribution, given the different composition (new low-MPL entrants vs. established incumbents).
MR-006
Q1c — Offer-dispersion σ is downward-truncated with no Flinn correction; λ=0.75 over-removes vs the stated measurement-error rationale
01h_nonemployed_pool.py (heckman_impute) / 00_config.py · Regression Specification
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
sd_all = float(ols.resid.std())                 # residual SD of ACCEPTED paid-hourly log wages
# config rationale: lambda 0.75 "nets out approximate measurement error (~0.10-0.15 log pts)"
ls = _OFFER_LAMBDA * sigma_g                     # 0.75 * sigma_g (linear scaling of SD)
Why This Is a Concern
Two compounding issues on the dispersion σ. (1) The SD used to spread offers is the residual SD of accepted wages, a below-truncated sample — the very low-wage offers that would push people below the $16.80 target are the ones least likely to be observed as accepted employment, so the estimated σ (overall 0.395; group range 0.29-0.55) is an understated proxy for the true offer-dispersion SD, and no Flinn (2006) / truncation correction reinflates it. (2) The λ=0.75 trim is justified in the config as netting out CPS measurement error (~0.10-0.15 log points), but measurement error should be removed in quadrature, not by proportional SD scaling. For the actual group σ range, the quadrature-consistent λ is 0.86-0.98 (computed: σ=0.395, me=0.15 → λ=0.925; σ=0.29, me=0.15 → λ=0.856), never 0.75. So λ=0.75 removes more variance than the stated rationale supports, adding a second downward tilt on top of the truncation. Both push MPL dispersion — and therefore reachability and entry — below where the disclosed reasoning would put it (feeds MR-001).
What to Check
  • Reconcile λ=0.75 with the measurement-error rationale using quadrature: if me≈0.10-0.15 log pts, the implied λ is ~0.86-0.98 — either raise λ or restate the rationale (there may be a separate real-heterogeneity argument, but it should be named).
  • Consider a Flinn-style truncation/selection correction to the offer-dispersion σ (the residual is from accepted wages, not offers), or at minimum disclose that σ is a lower bound on offer dispersion.
  • Because λ is already a 02f axis {0.50, 1.00}, make the headline λ and its justification explicit in the draft, not just in a config comment.

MR-007
No sampling uncertainty propagated; single-mother reachable cell rests on 4,576 unweighted observations
01h_nonemployed_pool.py / 02d_matching_simulation.py · Standard Errors
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
# diagnostics: reachable_N — single_mothers 4,576 | other_women 40,861 | men 25,949
# band = eps_ext scenario uncertainty only (ST-9); no bootstrap / replicate weights anywhere
Why This Is a Concern
The published band represents only eps_ext scenario spread (disclosed, ST-9); no sampling error is carried through the Heckman imputation, the λ-calibration, or the entry counts. The single-mothers cell — a headline demographic — is calibrated on just 4,576 unweighted reachable observations (weighted 1.57M) and produces only 0.13M entrants; its estimates (median g_net, calibrated λ, induced count) carry real sampling noise that is currently invisible. CPS is a complex survey and provides replicate weights for exactly this purpose; none are used, and the simulation is deterministic. For a public-facing number this understates the true uncertainty and can create false precision (e.g. reporting 0.13M single-mother entrants to two significant figures). This is separate from, and additional to, the scenario band.
What to Check
  • Propagate at least first-order sampling uncertainty (a bootstrap over pool rows, or CPS replicate weights) through the Heckman → calibration → entry chain, and report it alongside the scenario band.
  • Flag the thin single-mother reachable cell (N=4,576) explicitly and avoid over-precise reporting for it.
  • State clearly in the draft that the band is scenario-only, not a confidence interval, so readers do not read it as sampling uncertainty.

MR-008
Q4 — min(0.90,·) target cap is structurally non-binding; the 1.5 ceiling is the operative bound and increasingly binds at extreme variants
01h_nonemployed_pool.py (assign_reservation_band) / 00_config.py · Regression Specification
LOW
Relevant Code
target = min(0.90, float(np.average(_b.response_multiplier(eps, gr, _SAT_EXT) - 1.0, weights=wr)))
# _SAT_EXT = ceiling_ext = 1.50  =>  per-person (response_multiplier - 1) is bounded above by M-1 = 0.5
# so the weighted MEAN can never exceed 0.5 < 0.90  ->  the 0.90 cap never activates
Why This Is a Concern
Answering Q4 directly: the calibration does converge and realized≈target holds at all edges in the diagnostics (e.g. men central realized 0.0097 vs target 0.0096; single mothers upper 0.1015 vs 0.1011). Because ceiling_ext=1.5 caps each person's response_multiplier − 1 at 0.5, the weighted-mean target can never reach 0.90 — so the min(0.90, ·) guard is dead code that never binds and is misleading (it implies a 90% share ceiling that is structurally unreachable). The operative constraint is the 1.5 ceiling itself. At the extreme variants (pen20: 63.6% below target; skewstat_hi: 69.5%) more individuals have large g_net and are pushed toward their per-person 0.5 cap, so the saturating form increasingly compresses the upper tail — meaning those upper variants are themselves held down by ceiling_ext=1.5, which is never sensitivity-tested. If the true participation ceiling for a low-wage cell exceeds +50%, the upper band understates.
What to Check
  • Replace the min(0.90, ·) guard with the true structural bound or remove it, and document that ceiling_ext=1.5 (per-person cap 0.5) is the binding object.
  • Add a sensitivity on ceiling_ext (e.g. {1.5, 2.0}) since it is currently a fixed judgment bound that materially limits the upper variants.
  • Confirm per-variant realized≈target for pen20/skewstat_hi (their diagnostics JSONs are deleted by 02f cleanup; persist them, at least transiently, to verify convergence at the extremes).

MR-009
Heckman probit and wage OLS are estimated UNWEIGHTED; population MPL imputed from unweighted CPS coefficients; no replicate-weight SEs
01h_nonemployed_pool.py (heckman_impute) · Dataset Variable Usage
LOW
Relevant Code
probit = sm.Probit(D, Xsel.to_numpy()).fit(disp=0, maxiter=200)   # no weights
ols    = sm.OLS(y, Xw.to_numpy()).fit()                           # no weights
# but the pool below-target share and lambda-calibration DO use WTFINL
Why This Is a Concern
The selection probit and the wage OLS that generate every non-worker's imputed MPL are run unweighted, while the downstream reachability share and λ-calibration use WTFINL. CPS is a complex sample; unweighted coefficients estimate the model over the sample distribution, not the population, so the imputed potential-wage structure applied to the population pool may be biased if selection into the sample correlates with the wage residual (Solon-Haider-Wooldridge 2015 discuss when weighting is and is not warranted — but the choice should be deliberate and disclosed, not implicit). The weight variable used elsewhere (WTFINL, basic-monthly person weight) is the correct weight for person-level CPS, and the ORG earnings frame is handled via MISH 4/8 restriction — so this is a weighting-of-the-regression concern, not a wrong-weight-variable error. Separately, no replicate weights or design-based SEs are used anywhere (feeds MR-007).
What to Check
  • Decide and document whether the Heckman probit/OLS should be weighted (EARNWT for the ORG wage equation; WTFINL for the selection probit) or justify unweighted estimation explicitly.
  • Confirm the smearing factor (1.0777) and IMR coefficient (0.0755) are stable under weighting — a large shift would indicate weight-correlated misspecification.
  • If the survey design is declared (svydesign-equivalent), use replicate weights for the imputation-stage SEs.
