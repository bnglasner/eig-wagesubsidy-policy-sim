AS-001
"Accept re-employment at ~90% of prior wages" is not the finding of Krueger & Mueller (2016)
drafts/2026-07-08_wage-subsidy-impact-cost-summary.md (footnote 8) · Fabrication Detection
MEDIUM
What Was Claimed
Footnote 8: "Krueger, Alan B., and Andreas Mueller … AEJ: Economic Policy 8, no. 1 (2016): 142–179 (unemployed workers accept re-employment at roughly 90 percent of prior wages)."
What Was Found
The paper exists exactly as cited (verified: AEJ:EP 8(1):142–79, Feb 2016). But its actual result concerns reservation wages, not accepted wages: reservation wages decline ≈0.05–0.14% per week of unemployment and are anchored near the prior wage. The specific "accept re-employment at ~90% of prior wages" is an accepted-wage ratio that the paper does not report as its finding. Notably, the project's own config comment states it correctly ("Krueger-Mueller 2016 (reservation ~ prior wage)"), so this is a drafting slip in the prose, not a code error. The appendix Section 3 table also phrases it correctly ("reservation wages near prior wages").
How It Was Verified
WebSearch on the exact title returned the AEA/NBER/IZA records and the abstract; cross-referenced against 00_config.py:180 and appendix §3.
What to Check
  • Reword footnote 8 to "reservation wages near prior wages" (matching the config and appendix), or attach the ~90% accepted-wage figure to a source that actually reports it.
  • Confirm the SSP/Paycheck-Plus band edges do not lean on the misstated "90% accepted" reading.

AS-002
Sensitivities and the marginal cost-per-job are described as "evidence-central" but computed on the conservative-floor pool
02d_matching_simulation.py:437–511 / drafts (both) · Comment-Code Fidelity
MEDIUM
What Was Claimed
Main draft: "Three stress tests worth disclosing, applied to the evidence-central. If no married person with an employed spouse ever enters … the estimate falls by roughly a sixth. If take-up matches mature programs … every entry and cost figure scales down by a fifth." And in the cost-per-job section: the headline marginal cost is "$5,100 gross and $3,600 net per new worker" (evidence-central, $6.4B/1.25M), then "assigning hours independently … would roughly double … to around $9,600 gross."
What Was Found
02d computes entry_margin_band, entrant_hours_sensitivity, and entry_reconciliation on the canonical pool — penalty 0, λ 0.75, i.e. the 0.83M conservative floor — not on the evidence-central pool. The live outputs confirm: spouse-zeroed = 0.83→0.69M, take-up 0.80 = 0.83→0.66M, and the entrant-hours table runs at induced_M=0.83 (rank $4,018 → independent $9,576 gross per job). The percentage effects (−1/6, −1/5) are ~scale-invariant so transporting them to 1.25M is approximately valid, but: (a) the prose asserts the wrong basis, and (b) the "$5,100 → ~$9,600, roughly double" juxtaposition mixes an evidence-central numerator ($5,120) with a floor-pool sensitivity ratio; doubling $5,100 is ~$10,200, and the "$9,600" is actually double the floor's $4,000, not the headline's $5,100. The appendix pairs the numbers correctly ("~$9,600 gross vs ~$4,000"); the main draft does not.
How It Was Verified
Traced _entrants_from_pool / entrant_hours_sensitivity callers in 02d (pool = nonemployed_pool.parquet, the floor). Loaded entry_headline_scenarios.parquet, entrant_hours_sensitivity.parquet, and entry_margin_band.parquet with the project venv.
What to Check
  • Either recompute the household-coordination / take-up / entrant-hours sensitivities on the evidence-central pool, or change the prose to say the ratios are transported from the floor pool.
  • Fix the "roughly double to $9,600" sentence so the base and the doubled value share one basis (both floor, or both evidence-central).
  • Cross-reference doc-number-report for the exact per-job arithmetic.

AS-003
The 50% headline uplift (0.83M → 1.25M) rests on one un-pinned judgment lever; the claimed offsetting "two-sided" biases are never sized
02g_entry_scenario_grid.py / 00_config.py:266 / drafts (both) · Confidence Calibration
MEDIUM
What Was Claimed
The lede headlines "roughly 1.25 million non-workers drawn into jobs (a deliberately conservative floor sits near 0.8 million)." The justification: "we headline the evidence-central because a zero wage penalty is the one setting the evidence rules out … The true center is genuinely two-sided" (offsetting upward biases: instant matching, no childcare/commuting costs, no UI in the baseline).
What Was Found
The entire 0.83M→1.25M move is produced by one lever — the status-differentiated non-employment wage penalty (config evidence_central_status_penalty). The existence of a penalty is evidence-backed (Schmieder-von Wachter-Bender decay; Krueger-Mueller anchoring — both verified real), but its specific magnitude (5/10/15%, pool-weighted ≈11%) is disclosed judgment, and it happens to be the setting that lifts the headline 50% above the model's own floor. The countervailing "two-sided" biases (childcare/commuting frictions, instant matching) that would pull the number down are asserted but never quantified — no attempt is made to size whether they offset the penalty uplift, the still-conservative λ=0.75, or each other. So "evidence-central" carries more authority than the evidence supports for the specific value 1.25M versus, say, 1.0M or 1.4M. This is a framing/calibration concern, not an arithmetic error: the number is a faithful, reproducible model output; the question is whether headlining it (rather than the floor, or a two-sided interval) is warranted. Mitigating: the floor, the high corner, the full 0.18–3.37M grid, and a "Medium" confidence label are all shown prominently.
How It Was Verified
Read 02g's three-bundle construction and the config re-center block; confirmed against entry_headline_scenarios.parquet (floor 0.83 / evidence-central 1.25 / high 3.37) and the diagnostics JSON (penalty applied via EIG_MPL_STATUS_PENALTY). Verified the penalty citations exist by WebSearch.
What to Check
  • Add at least a rough magnitude for the downward "two-sided" biases (childcare/commuting, matching frictions) so the reader can judge whether they plausibly offset the +50% penalty uplift.
  • Consider presenting the headline as an interval (e.g. floor-to-central) rather than a single 1.25M point, given the driver is a judgment penalty.
  • State explicitly that the 50% gap between the floor and the headline is one lever, not an accumulation of evidence.

AS-006
renegotiated_wage() invariant "the subsidy cannot push the cash wage up" is violated below the $7.25 floor (latent)
02d_matching_simulation.py:70–77 · Edge-Case Stress Tests
LOW
What Was Claimed
Line 75 comment: "the subsidy cannot push the cash wage up" — implemented as w1 = min(w1, w0) then w1 = max(w1, FED_MIN).
What Was Found
Because the max(·, FED_MIN) floor is applied after the min(·, w0) cap, an input wage below $7.25 is lifted to $7.25 > w0, silently violating the stated cap. Test: w0=$5.00, β=0.5 → w1=$7.25 (take-home $14.89 > w0). This does not bind on real data — imputed MPL is floored at $7.25 in 01h (np.maximum(FED_MIN, …)) before any entrant renegotiation, and incumbent employer wages are at/above the floor — so the observed outputs are correct. It is a latent invariant break worth a one-line guard or comment.
How It Was Verified
Self-contained numeric test of renegotiated_wage across β∈{0.3,0.5,0.7} and w0∈{5, 7.25, 10, 16.80, 25} in the project venv; traced the upstream MPL floor in 01h heckman_impute / penalty steps.
What to Check
  • If sub-floor wages could ever reach this function, cap after flooring (w1 = min(max(w1, FED_MIN), w0) only when w0 ≥ FED_MIN) or assert w0 ≥ FED_MIN.
