## Q1 — Net-of-transfer viability formula (plan Open Decision #3)

**Proposed:** entrant viable iff `NI(employer_earned + subsidy) − NI(0) ≥ NI(r_i × hours) − NI(0)`, with `NI()` the PolicyEngine schedule already used in `_entrant_fiscal`, and `r_i` the gross-basis reservation wage calibrated in `01h::assign_reservation`.

**Verdict: FLAWED-FIXABLE.** The comparison is conditionally coherent as economics, but wiring it in as proposed breaks the model's own calibration identity, and several counterfactual-income omissions and a cliff non-monotonicity need explicit handling.

### (a) Is the comparison economically correct?

Conditionally yes. Read `r_i` as "the gross wage at which, run through the household's actual tax-and-transfer schedule, the person is indifferent between working `h` hours and not working." Then the indifference condition is `U(NI(r·h), not working) = U(NI(0), not working) + disutility terms`, and the money-metric of the full cost of working (disutility of effort + forgone home production + childcare + lost leisure) is exactly `NI(r·h) − NI(0)`. Acceptance requires the net gain of the offered package to exceed that: `NI(w_e·h + s·h) − NI(0) ≥ NI(r·h) − NI(0)`. Since the `NI(0)` terms cancel, this reduces to `NI(package) ≥ NI(r·h)` — a clean, well-defined gate. Two conditions must hold for this reading to be licensed:

1. `r_i` must genuinely be a *gross-wage-equivalent under the same schedule* reservation concept. In `01h` it is not an elicited or estimated quantity — it is a calibration device (`r = mpl × (1+m)`) whose distribution was solved to hit a **gross** viable-share target. See (b): the license is currently absent.
2. `hours` must be the same on both sides of the comparison and in the fiscal step. With W6's quantile-matched entrant hours this is satisfiable but must be asserted, not assumed — a per-hour `r_i` times full-time hours against a part-time offered package would mis-gate part-time entrants. The proportional-in-hours reservation also embeds "no fixed cost of work," which is exactly the deferred M6 gap; the plan already discloses this, and the disclosure must carry over to the formula's documentation (a fixed cost enters as an intercept, not a slope, so `NI(r·h) − NI(0)` proportional to `h` understates the cost of work for short-hours offers — most severely for single mothers).

One further disclosure (LOW, fold into ST-3): `NI(employer_earned + subsidy)` runs the subsidy through the schedule as ordinary earned income — i.e., it is implicitly **taxable and countable against means-tested benefits**. That is a policy-design assumption, not a neutral computation. If the 80-80 subsidy is designed to be excluded from means-tested program income (as the source articles' "no phase-out" framing suggests), this understates the net gain and the induced entry. State the assumption in `docs/entry_from_nonemployment_methodology.md` either way.

### (b) The calibration/gate inconsistency — **ST-1 (HIGH). Real, and the plan must resolve it, not inherit it.**

Confirmed as a genuine internal inconsistency. `01h::assign_reservation` bisects `λ_c` so that the **gross** criterion `y + s(y) ≥ r` holds for exactly `target_share = min(0.90, eps_ext_c × mean(g_c))` of the weighted reachable pool. If `02d::_entrants_from_pool` then re-gates on the net criterion, the induced-entry share no longer equals the calibration target **even at the central point of the band** — the model stops reproducing its own anchor. Worse, the wedge is **cell-differential in a perverse direction**: net-of-transfer gains are smallest where effective marginal tax rates from benefit phase-outs are largest — means-tested households with children, i.e., precisely the `single_mothers` cell with the highest `eps_ext`. So the net gate would silently shrink the cell the calibration says responds most, while barely touching `men`. The published entry numbers would then match neither the literature anchor nor a disclosed alternative — the worst of both.

**Right fix (recommended): calibrate the bisection itself on the net criterion.** Inside the λ-solve, the viability indicator becomes `1{NI(package_i) ≥ NI(r_i(λ)·h_i)}`. Monotonicity of the bisection survives: as λ rises, markups `m = −log(1−u)/λ` fall pointwise, `r` falls, `NI(r·h)` is (weakly, up to cliff-local exceptions) non-increasing, so the aggregate viable share is non-decreasing in λ — the bisection still converges, with at most cliff-induced flat spots (see (d); use the share, not a person-level root). Cost: `01h` acquires a dependency on the `02b` schedule machinery (`_resolve_schedule` / `_ni_adjusted`) and on the pool's `family_type_key`/`state_code` — an architectural change the plan should schedule explicitly (it currently sequences W5 before W6 as if they were independent; under this fix W5 needs the schedule plumbing).

**Second-best (acceptable only with disclosure): decouple target from gate.** Keep the gross-basis λ calibration as the definition of the `r` distribution, apply the net gate downstream, and *report the per-cell calibration wedge* (gross-calibrated share vs. net-gated realized share, by cell and band edge) as a mandatory diagnostic table. This is defensible under the spec's own philosophy (elasticities as priors, not equalities), but it materially weakens the interpretability of the band: an `eps_ext` of 0.50 no longer maps to anything a reader can check. If this route is taken, `entry_margin_band.parquet` must carry both the calibrated and realized shares.

**ST-2 (MEDIUM) — stimulus base consistency.** The EITC/CBO participation elasticities the band anchors on are estimated with respect to the **net-of-tax-and-transfer return to work** (Meyer & Rosenbaum 2001 model participation directly on net incomes; the CBO/McClelland-Mok review elasticities are net-return concepts). `01h` currently applies them to the mean **gross** proportional gain `g`. If the gate moves to a net basis (ST-1), the stimulus in the target (`mean(g)`) should move with it — otherwise the calibration multiplies a net-return elasticity by a gross-return stimulus, which overstates the target share whenever EMTRs are positive. This also interacts with W2b: the SSP and Paycheck Plus unit conversions (`eps ≈ Δemployment / mean_g`) must use the same (net or gross) basis chosen here, or the band edges will be internally inconsistent with their own central point. The plan's W2b text currently computes `g` on a gross earnings basis; flag and align.

### (c) What the `NI(0)` counterfactual misses — **ST-3 (MEDIUM)**

Checked against the code: `_entrant_fiscal` computes `NI(i_policy) − NI(0) − employer_earned`, where `_ni_adjusted` interpolates `net_income` on the earned-income axis and adds back `aca_ptc` and `medicaid_chip`. So means-tested benefits at zero earned income (SNAP, TANF, Medicaid value) **are** in `NI(0)` — the biggest worry is already handled. What is genuinely missing:

1. **UI benefits.** The CPS non-employed pool includes the unemployed, a nontrivial share of whom receive UI. UI is not on an earned-income schedule axis and is absent from `NI(0)`. Understating counterfactual income (i) overstates the net gain from work → overstates entry under the net gate, and (ii) overstates the fiscal delta in the *wrong* direction for costs — the government's UI savings when a recipient takes a job are not credited, so net cost per UI-recipient entrant is overstated. The two biases do not cancel; they both distort. Minimum fix: split the pool by a UI-plausible flag (EMPSTAT unemployed vs. NILF) and disclose; better, add a stylized UI term for the unemployed with a disclosed replacement rate and duration.
2. **Monthly status vs. annual schedule.** Pool membership is a survey-month state (weight `WTFINL/n_months` converts person-months to an average stock); the schedule is annual. Treating a June-nonemployed person as `NI(0)` for the whole year overstates both the net gain to entry and the fiscal delta for anyone with part-year earnings (very common among the non-employed — labor-force entrants/exiters). Disclose; if feasible, sensitivity with a part-year (e.g., half-year) counterfactual earnings assumption.
3. **Household keying.** The schedule is keyed by the coarse `family_type_key` (`single/married × 0c/2c`) with template spouse earnings baked into whatever `_resolve_schedule` returns for "married." Real spouse earnings shift both `NI(0)` and the phase-out region the entrant's earnings land in. The W4 ASEC-match household fields (or better, the real in-CPS rosters — see Q2c) improve this; the plan already notes it. What it should also note: `NI(r·h) − NI(0)` conflates the *transfer-schedule* component of the cost of work with the *home-production/leisure* component. That is fine under the reading in (a) — but it means any error in the schedule (wrong spouse income, missing UI) propagates directly into the imputed disutility of work, since `r` was calibrated to a share, not to a preference parameter. This is another reason ST-1's "calibrate on the net criterion" fix is preferable: it keeps the calibration and the schedule errors on the same basis so they cannot compound.

### (d) Cliff non-monotonicity — **ST-4 (MEDIUM). Real; the formula is well-defined but the wage-setting logic around it breaks.**

`_ni_adjusted` adds ACA PTC and Medicaid/CHIP *into* net income, so their eligibility cliffs (and SNAP/TANF cliffs inside `net_income` itself) appear as discontinuous **drops** in `NI` as gross income rises — `NI()` is not monotone. Consequences:

- Yes, a person can be net-viable at a low wage and non-viable at a higher one. The proposed formula evaluated at the single bargained wage `w_e` is well-defined and "handles" this in the trivial sense, but it interacts badly with `_entrants_from_pool`'s existing repair step `w_e = where(takehome < r, min(y, r), w_e)`, which pays up to clear the **gross** reservation. On a net basis, "pay up" can cross a cliff and *reduce* the worker's net position — the repair can flip a viable match to non-viable. A net-basis clearing wage may not exist in `[w_e, y]`, may not be unique, and is not found by monotone search.
- Recommended handling: evaluate net viability at the offered package; if it fails, evaluate at the *best* package over a small grid of `w ∈ [w_e, y]` (the schedule is already gridded — reuse its income axis) and take the max-net point; count and report the number of entrants whose viability is non-monotone in `w` (a "cliff diagnostic"). If the count is small, a footnote suffices; if large, it is substantive and belongs in the write-up (the source articles' no-cliff argument is *about* exactly this).
- The bisection in ST-1's fix should target the aggregate share (weighted mean of indicators), which is monotone in λ up to cliff-local noise; do not root-find person-level.

## Q2 — Spouse income as Heckman exclusion restriction (plan W4)

**Verdict: FLAWED-RETHINK** for the exclusion-restriction use of nearest-neighbor-matched spouse income. The ASEC-match extension remains worthwhile for household-composition precision in the NI schedules (M5) — keep W4 for that purpose — but do **not** put the matched spouse income in the selection equation as an identifying instrument. Separately, the binding weakness of the current Heckman is not the exclusion restriction at all (see ST-7).

### (a) Generic critique — applies even to *real* spouse income

Assortative mating on education, ability, and earnings capacity is first-order in US data: spouse income correlates with own unobserved wage-equation error, violating the exclusion. This is the classic critique of "non-wife income" instruments in the female-labor-supply literature — Mroz (1987) showed participation-equation estimates are highly sensitive to which exclusions are assumed, and the tradition since treats husband's income as a *conventional but contestable* participation shifter, not a clean instrument. So even the best-case version of W4 needs a disclosure paragraph, not a validity claim.

### (b) The specific fatal problem — **ST-5 (HIGH). Confirmed; the reasoning in the question is correct.**

Walk through what the matched variable *is*. `01e`'s match runs on six covariates: `wage_decile` (weight 0.35 — the largest), `marital_binary`, `nchild_bin`, `age_bin`, `sex_binary`, `educ_group`. For the non-employed pool, `wage_decile` does not exist (no wage), so the mirrored `01i` match would effectively run on {marital, nchild, age, sex, educ} — which is, item for item, a subset of the covariates already in the selection design matrix `Xsel` (`_design(wage_eq=False)` contains age, age², educ dummies, male, race/ethnicity, married, nchild, has_child). The matched `asec_spouse_income` is therefore `f(X_sel-cells) + matching noise`: within a match cell it is (nearly) constant at the nearest donor's value; across cells it varies only because `X_sel` varies. It carries **no identifying variation beyond `X_sel`**:

- The systematic component is a (step-function) transformation of regressors already in the selection equation. Adding it is adding a nonlinear function of included `X` — identification then rests entirely on functional form, i.e., on the nonlinearity of the inverse Mills ratio. That is precisely the configuration Puhani (2000) shows to be fragile: without genuine excluded variation, the IMR is close to linear over most of its support, the wage equation is near-collinear in `(X, IMR)`, and the selection coefficient is identified off distributional assumptions alone.
- The idiosyncratic component — *which* ASEC donor happened to be nearest in the ball tree — is matching arbitrariness, not economic variation in the person's household budget. Treating it as exogenous variation in the participation incentive is not an assumption anyone can defend; it is noise dressed as an instrument. It will inflate apparent precision (the probit will fit a coefficient on it) without adding identification content — strictly worse than honestly reporting identification-by-nonlinearity, because it *conceals* it.

**ST-6 (HIGH) — a mechanical artifact the plan does not mention.** The Heckman selection probit is fit on `est` (the MISH 4/8 rotation sample), which contains both employed and non-employed rows. To put spouse income in `Xsel`, every `est` row needs a value. If employed rows get `01e`-style matches (which use `wage_decile`) and non-employed rows get `01i`-style matches (which cannot), the constructed regressor differs *systematically by the selection outcome `D` itself* — it would mechanically predict selection and produce a spurious, meaningless probit coefficient. If instead all rows are matched without `wage_decile` for symmetry, the variable collapses fully to case (b) above. There is no version of the NN-matched spouse income that works as an exclusion restriction. **Recommended plan change: strike "add spouse income to `Xsel` as a new exclusion restriction" from W5; retain the `01i` match only as a household-composition input to M5's schedule keying (where matching-on-observables is exactly the right tool).**

### (c) Real in-CPS spouse variables (ORG rosters) — the superior alternative, with honest caveats

Building real spouse links from SERIAL/PERNUM/RELATE (all rotations) gives **actual spouse employment status** for every married-spouse-present person in the pool and estimation sample, and real spouse earnings for the MISH 4/8 subset. As an exclusion restriction:

- **Spouse employment (recommended primary):** genuine person-level variation, identically constructed for `D=0` and `D=1` rows (no ST-6 artifact), available for all rotations. The identifying assumption — spouse's employment shifts own participation (income effect, added-worker effect, coordinated leisure) but not own *offered wage* conditional on age/education/race/state — is the standard one in the applied female-labor-supply literature descending from Heckman (1974, 1979) and Mroz (1987), where husband's income/wage is the canonical participation shifter. The assortative-mating critique from (a) still applies (spouse's employment correlates with own unobserved earning capacity through sorting), so it is *defensible with disclosure*, not clean. Two mitigations worth doing: (i) enter it interacted with `married` (it is structurally undefined for singles — do not let the zero-for-unmarried coding masquerade as variation); (ii) run the falsification regression of log wage on spouse employment conditional on `X` among the employed — not a proof of exclusion, but a cheap red-flag detector.
- **Spouse earnings (MISH 4/8 only):** stronger economics but only ~2/8 of rotations; using it would either shrink the estimation sample or reintroduce a constructed-differently-by-subsample problem. Use spouse employment; keep earnings for a sensitivity.

### (d) Children under 5 — **the more standard shifter; use it alongside, with its own caveat**

Presence of a child under 5/6 (IPUMS: `NCHLT5`, or `YNGCH < 5` — derivable or re-pullable) is the textbook participation-equation shifter (Mroz 1987 uses children <6 and 6–18; the entire applied Heckman tradition follows). It is sharper than the current `NCHILD`/`has_child` because the participation constraint is concentrated in pre-school ages. Caveat to disclose: the motherhood wage *penalty* (Waldfogel 1997; Budig & England 2001) means children arguably belong in the wage equation for women, so "children affect participation but not wages" is contestable — as is the current design's exclusion of `married` from the wage equation, given the well-documented male marriage wage premium (Korenman & Neumark 1991). **Note this cuts at the *current* code too (ST-8, MEDIUM):** `01h::_design` already excludes `married` and `nchild` from the wage equation, so the existing Heckman is identified off exclusions with known direct wage associations. The honest position, for the docs file W1 creates: *no bulletproof exclusion restriction exists in these data*; the chosen set (spouse employment × married, child-under-5) is the conventional best available; report a Puhani-style sensitivity (Heckman vs. plain OLS-on-employed imputation vs. IMR-in-wage-equation-dropped) so readers see how much the correction moves the pool MPL distribution.

### (e) Is the Heckman the binding weakness? — **ST-7 (HIGH). No — the prediction step is, and the plan's diagnostics would miss it.**

The red flag (pool MPL median $21.00 == employed median; IMR coefficient +0.296) is substantially explained by a modeling choice in `heckman_impute`, not by weak exclusions. The code predicts the pool's potential wage with the Mills term set to zero (`Xwp["imr"] = 0.0`), i.e., it returns the **unconditional** population mean `E[log w | X]`. But the estimated `+0.296 = ρσ > 0` says workers are positively selected on unobservables — and therefore the non-employed are *negatively* selected on unobservables. The selection-theory-consistent imputation for a non-employed person is the **conditional** mean

`E[log w | X, D=0] = Xβ + ρσ · λ₀(Xγ)`, with `λ₀ = −φ(Xγ)/(1−Φ(Xγ)) < 0`,

which sits strictly *below* `Xβ`. Setting the Mills term to zero deliberately discards the very negative selection the docstring says the method exists to capture ("places the non-employed below the observed-worker distribution, as selection theory implies" — with Mills = 0 it does so only through observables, which evidently do not differ enough to move the median). There is a coherent argument for Mills = 0 — if one believes the unobservable is pure reservation-wage heterogeneity (taste), not productivity, then potential MPL is `Xβ` — but that argument contradicts the file's own stated rationale, and it is exactly the assumption that makes the pool look as productive as the employed. **Recommended plan change (W5): add the `D=0` conditional Mills term to the pool prediction (or run both and report the gap as the headline diagnostic), *before* investing in new exclusion restrictions.** With `ρσ = +0.296`, the conditional correction is first-order: for a person with participation index at the median of the non-employed, the downward shift is on the order of `0.296 × λ₀` log points — plausibly the entire missing "negative selection" the M4 plausibility check is looking for. The exclusion-restriction work (c)/(d) then serves its proper role: making `ρσ` and `λ₀` credible, rather than being asked to fix a symptom the prediction step created. Also note the Duan smearing factor is estimated on employed residuals and applied to the pool — fine under homoskedastic transferability, worth one disclosure line.

## Q3 — Band mechanism and rank reuse (plan W5)

### (a) Rank reuse across band edges — **SOUND. ST-9 (LOW, disclosure only).**

Reusing the same per-person rank `u` across lower/central/upper edges, with only `λ_c` re-solved, is the right construction *for what this band is*: parameter (scenario) uncertainty, not sampling uncertainty. It is the common-random-numbers principle: holding the individual-level draw fixed isolates the parameter's effect, eliminates Monte Carlo noise from the band comparison, and yields nested viable sets — which is the economically natural statement "a higher participation elasticity means a superset of the same people enter." Independent draws per edge would *add* spurious compositional churn to the band without representing any real uncertainty. Two notes:

1. **The plan's verification step 1 is wrong in a helpful direction:** it says `reservation_lower ≥ central ≥ upper` monotonicity is "not guaranteed by construction — check." With shared `u`, it **is** guaranteed: the bisected share is non-decreasing in λ, so a higher `eps_ext` target ⇒ higher `λ` ⇒ `m = −log(1−u)/λ` lower *pointwise* ⇒ reservation columns ordered pointwise, viable sets nested. Assert it in the test suite; a violation would indicate a bug, not a modeling choice. (The `min(0.90, ·)` cap and the `target_share ≤ 0 → λ=1e-6` branch preserve the ordering.)
2. **Disclosure (ST-9):** the band represents *only* `eps_ext` scenario uncertainty. It excludes sampling error, the exponential-markup functional-form assumption, and who-is-marginal heterogeneity (the fixed rank means the identity of marginal entrants is deterministic). One sentence in the W7 docs prevents the band being read as a confidence interval.

### (b) Row-order rank assignment — **ST-10 (HIGH). Real compositional bias; the plan misses it; the fix is trivial.**

Confirmed from code, not conjecture: `u = (arange(len(gr)) + 0.5) / len(gr)` is assigned in **row order** over the reachable subset within each cell. The row order of the pool is deterministic and non-random: `01h` reads the year partitions sorted (`sorted(raw_dir.glob(...))[-2:]`) and concatenates, and the ingestion stage (`00a`/`01a`) writes each year partition in IPUMS delivery order with no reshuffle (verified: `00a` only `arrange(sample_date)`; `01a` subsets by year mask and writes as-is). IPUMS CPS extracts are ordered by sample (YEAR, MONTH) and, within month, by household SERIAL in the source-file order. Therefore, with certainty, **`u` is increasing in survey year and month**: every reachable 2025 person in a cell receives a lower rank — hence a systematically lower markup `m` and lower reservation wage — than every 2026 person. The viable set is mechanically overweighted toward the earlier survey year and earlier months. Within month, whether SERIAL order clusters by state I could not verify empirically (no pandas-capable interpreter available in this environment); it is plausible for CPS source files, and if present it produces exactly the low-FIPS-states-get-low-markups geographic skew the question describes. The time-dimension skew alone is sufficient to establish the defect.

Does it matter? Yes, materially in principle: the cell-level *aggregate* viable share is calibrated correctly by construction, but *who* fills it is an artifact of file order. `02d` prices entrants through **state-keyed, family-type-keyed** fiscal schedules (`_entrant_fiscal` groups by `(family_type_key, state_code)`), so entrant composition — geography, and anything correlated with survey timing (seasonal composition of the non-employed, year-to-year demographic drift) — feeds directly into net cost, state-level results, and the by-cell entry table. The error is silent, deterministic, and stable across reruns, so it will pass the plan's proposed determinism check while being wrong.

**Fix — confirmed correct, with a preference:** assign `u` by a *seeded random permutation* within cell, or (preferable) by ranking a **hash of a stable person identifier** (YEAR-MONTH-SERIAL-PERNUM). Both preserve the exact marginal distribution of `u` (a permutation of the same grid), so the λ-bisection and the calibrated aggregate share are unchanged; both restore independence of `m` from row position in expectation; both are deterministic. The hash version is strictly better: it is invariant to row reordering and to pool-size changes (adding a year does not reshuffle everyone's markup), which protects the delta/replication note in W8. The permutation version must reuse `cfg["seed"]` and is only stable while input order is stable — acceptable, second-best. Whichever is chosen, reuse the *same* permuted/hashed `u` across the three band edges to retain Q3a's comonotonicity.

### (c) The docstring claim "m drawn INDEPENDENT of g" — **false as written; part of ST-10**

Under row-order assignment, `m_i` is a deterministic increasing function of row position among reachable persons. Independence from `g_i` holds only if row position is independent of `g_i` — but `g_i` is a deterministic function of the imputed MPL, which varies with demographics that are ordered in the file (year/month with certainty; geography plausibly). So independence is an unverified assertion that the data layout likely violates; with the hash/permutation fix it becomes true in expectation, and the docstring becomes honest. Note the viability condition `u ≤ 1−exp(−λ g_i)` *should* couple viability probability to `g` (higher-gain people more likely viable — that is the model); the defect is only the additional, unintended coupling to file position.

## Q4 — Calibration form consistency (linear 01h vs. saturating 02b) — **ST-11 (HIGH). FLAWED-FIXABLE.**

Confirmed internal inconsistency, in the direction of **overstating** induced entry. `01h` calibrates `target_share = min(0.90, eps_ext × mean(g))` — linear in `g` — while `02b` applies the Michaelis-Menten multiplier `m(g) = 1 + (M−1)·εg/((M−1)+εg)` with `M = ceiling_ext = 1.5`, *specifically because* (per `00_config.py`'s own comments) "the linear eps*g approximation likely OVERSTATES responses — especially the extensive margin" at the large `g` this subsidy generates (`g = 0.8·(16.80−y)/y` exceeds 1.0 as `y → 7.25`). The saturating form is strictly below the linear form for all `εg > 0`; the wedge is large exactly where the model says entry concentrates. Example at the central scenario: single mothers, `ε = 0.5`, `g = 0.5` → linear share 0.25 vs. saturating 0.167 — a 33 percent overstatement; at `g = 1.0` → 0.50 vs. 0.25 — 50 percent. The `min(0.90, ·)` cap is not a substitute for saturation (it binds only in absurd territory). There is also a second-order aggregation inconsistency: `01h` applies ε to the cell *mean* of `g` (nonlinearity after averaging), while `02b` applies the response person-level and then aggregates; for the concave saturating form, Jensen's inequality makes these differ (mean of `m(g)` < `m(mean g)`), so even matched functional forms would disagree unless `01h` moves to the person-level-then-aggregate convention.

**Recommended plan change (W5):** set the bisection target to the weighted person-level saturated response, `target_share_c = Σ w_i·(m(ε_c, g_i, M_ext) − 1) / Σ w_i` over reachable persons — same `response_multiplier` function `02b` already exports (and which `02d`'s *synthetic* fallback already uses, so the pool path is currently the odd one out). This makes the structural model consistent with the project's own stated extrapolation rationale and with both other entry-margin code paths.

**Direction for the "men" band debate:** this issue cuts **opposite** to the challenge prompt's worry. The worry is that the model understates men's entry (EITC-derived near-zero ε); this finding says the *level* of entry is likely overstated for every cell, most for high-`εg` cells. Two consequences: (i) fixing it lowers headline induced-entry and cost numbers, concentrated in `single_mothers`; (ii) because men's `εg` is small (ε = 0.05, near-linear region), saturation barely touches them — so men's *share* of induced entrants rises modestly under the consistent form. The two debates are separable and should be kept so in the write-up: the men question is about ε's central value (an evidence question, resolved by the spec as "stay anchored, widen the upper edge"); this question is about the response *function* (an internal-consistency question with a determinate answer).

## Q5 — 02b/02d scope split (challenge item 8)

**Verdict: SOUND with conditions.** The split is coherent in principle; the plan misses one real coupling and needs labeling discipline.

### (a) Two entry-margin numbers from two calibrations — **defensible, with a reconciliation obligation (part of ST-12, MEDIUM)**

Publishing a reduced-form benchmark (02b: "if the subsidy behaves as the EITC/CBO literature implies") alongside a structural estimate (02d) is standard and honest practice — *provided* the two are labeled as answers to different questions and the divergence between them is explained, not left for readers to discover. The current divergence sources are: different populations (02b's "entrants" are up-weighted incumbents via `weight_mult`; 02d's are actual non-employed pool members), different response forms (02b saturating; 02d-pool currently linear — ST-11), different stimulus bases (gross in both today; 02d moving to a net gate under M5 — ST-1/ST-2), and soon different elasticity sources (`scenarios` vs. `eps_ext_band`). Requirement: a small reconciliation table (02b central induced-M vs. 02d central induced-M, with the wedge attributed to population / functional form / net gate / elasticity source) in the W7 reporting. Without it, two headline entry numbers from "the same elasticities" is a review liability; with it, it is a strength.

### (b) `_entrants_synthetic` coupling — **ST-13 (MEDIUM). Confirmed; the plan misses it.**

`02d::_entrants_synthetic` (line 196) reads `cfg["behavioral"]["scenarios"]["central"]["eps_ext"]` directly and applies `response_multiplier`. Under the plan, the pool path would read `cfg["matching"]["eps_ext_band"]` while the fallback path silently reads the old namespace — so the model's entry margin would depend on elasticity values from *different config sections depending on whether `nonemployed_pool.parquet` exists on disk*. That is exactly the kind of silent environment-dependent divergence the band work is meant to eliminate. Also note `01h::assign_reservation` itself (line 224) reads `cfg["behavioral"]["scenarios"]["central"]` today — the plan does cover repointing that one (W5), but the fallback is unmentioned. Fix options: (i) repoint `_entrants_synthetic` at `eps_ext_band["central"]`; or (ii) make the fallback loudly labeled in output (`entrant_source = "synthetic (benchmark eps)"`) and exclude it from `entry_margin_band.parquet`; recommend both. Additionally, ensure `entry_margin_band.parquet` is only produced on the pool path — a band built by re-solving λ has no meaning in the synthetic path, which has no λ.

### (c) Recommended relabeling for 02b — **ST-14 (LOW)**

1. Rename the scenario axis in 02b's output/tables to make the benchmark role explicit: e.g., `benchmark-lower/central/upper (EITC-CBO reduced form)` — and reserve bare `lower/central/upper` for the 02d band, avoiding a name collision between `cfg["behavioral"]["scenarios"]["central"]` and `cfg["matching"]["eps_ext_band"]["central"]` (two different objects a reader will conflate).
2. Add a basis line to 02b's table notes: "gross-wage stimulus, saturating response, incumbent-weight entrants; not the structural entry estimate — see 02d."
