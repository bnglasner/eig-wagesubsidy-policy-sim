# Requirements Specification: Rethinking entry from nonemployment (extensive + intensive margins)

**Date:** 2026-07-08 (revised twice same day: after first evaluation round, then after a targeted second literature-scout pass on the household-composition channel)
**Status:** REVISED — evaluation complete (two rounds); ready for user approval to proceed to an implementation plan
**Save Location:** `Infrastructure/specs/2026-07-08_entry-from-nonemployment-remodel.md`

## Objective

Redesign how `code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py` and `code/02_descriptive_analysis/02d_matching_simulation.py` model entry from nonemployment (extensive margin: who enters; intensive margin: how many hours) so that EITC-literature elasticities **inform** the model — as priors, plausibility bounds, or validation targets — rather than **mechanically dictate** it via a hard calibration equality. This spec is the artifact to be stress-tested by `literature-scout` and `data-dictionary-agent` before any code changes; **no implementation happens under this spec** — a follow-on implementation spec is written after this one is approved.

## Rationale: why the current calibration is in tension with the project's own published argument

Two EIG Agglomerations pieces make the policy case for the 80-80 wage subsidy over the EITC:

- ["How to End Low-Wage Work Forever"](https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/how-to-end-low-wage-work-forever) — argues the EITC's demonstrated labor-supply effect is concentrated on the participation margin for women/single mothers and near-zero for childless adults and men (citing Meyer 2002), and attributes this **to the EITC's own institutional design**: annual lump-sum payment (not "real pay" in the worker's eyes), formula complexity that obscures the return to work, and a phase-out that raises effective marginal tax rates. The subsidy is explicitly designed to remove each of these frictions — per-paycheck payment, transparent formula, no phase-out cliff, and no categorical (child) conditioning.
- ["...Part 2: The FAQ"](https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/how-to-end-low-wage-work-forever-4a4) — reinforces this with a reservation-wage framing: workers stay out of the labor force when their reservation wage exceeds available offers; the subsidy narrows that gap directly and "without discrimination between workers with children and workers without them." It also flags reservation wages as a *net* household concept, and notes the wage subsidy avoids the EITC's cliff/complexity precisely so that the *effective* wage gain is more certain and legible to the worker.

**The tension:** `01h::assign_reservation` (see `code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py`) solves, per matching cell, for a reservation-markup rate `λ_c` such that

```
target_share_c = min(0.90, eps_ext_c × mean(gain_c))
```

is satisfied by construction, where `eps_ext = {single_mothers: 0.50, other_women: 0.20, men: 0.05}` (central scenario, `code/00_setup/00_config.py`) is sourced from EITC/CBO estimates (see the config's own comments: "CBO central," "Kleven-haircut," "CBO upper"). Because the equality is solved exactly, **the induced-entry count for every cell is, by construction, capped at the EITC's own demographic pattern** — including the near-zero response for men that the articles attribute to the EITC's *specific* institutional frictions, not to some invariant fact about male labor supply. A policy instrument built to differ from the EITC on exactly this margin is being evaluated with the EITC's own ceiling. This also creates an internal inconsistency: `02d`'s wage/bargaining side already draws on non-EITC structural theory (Mortensen-Pissarides 1994, Hosios 1990, Shimer 2005, Rothstein 2010, Hall-Milgrom 2008, Flinn 2006) — the entry *trigger* is the only piece of the "structural" model that is 100% EITC-derived and solved to a point equality rather than emergent from the structure.

This spec's purpose is to find a defensible way to let EITC evidence remain part of the evidence base (it is real, relevant, and directionally informative) without letting it be the thing that *produces* the number, especially for entry from nonemployment.

## Evaluation findings summary (2026-07-08)

Both `literature-scout` and `data-dictionary-agent` completed their assessments. **The headline finding is important and cuts against the working hypothesis, not toward it:** the additional evidence does **not** license simply assuming the wage subsidy will pull in more entrants (especially men) than the EITC's own pattern implies.

**Literature (full sourcing in Evidence, below).** Asked directly whether the EITC's muted response is decomposable into an "EITC-design-specific" component (fixable by a differently-designed subsidy) versus a "general labor-supply-constraint" component (would persist regardless of instrument design), `literature-scout`'s answer was **not cleanly separable — treat EITC elasticities as a soft prior/band, not a decomposable target.** Two genuine non-EITC wage-subsidy studies actively counsel caution: **Huttunen, Pirttilä & Uusitalo (2013)**, a Finnish employer-side wage subsidy, found **no extensive-margin effect**; **Bonin, Kempe & Schneider (2003)**, a German Kombilohn wage-subsidy microsimulation, found only **marginal** participation gains. **Chetty & Saez (2013)**'s randomized EITC information/salience experiment found a **null** effect, undercutting the simple "opacity mutes the EITC" mechanism the source articles lean on. The one real counterweight is **Card & Hyslop (2005)** (Canadian Self-Sufficiency Project, already cataloged), an actual wage-fill experiment with a larger participation response than EITC estimates — a genuine upper-bound data point, not a decomposition. **Conclusion: this supports Candidate Direction 1 (prior/band) over Direction 2 (structural decomposition) — there is no literature-licensed way to build separate salience/no-cliff/non-categorical multipliers.** The honest use of the broader evidence base is to widen the uncertainty band (Card-Hyslop-informed upper edge for women; Huttunen/Bonin reinforcing a near-zero lower edge for men), not to raise the central estimate.

**Data feasibility (full sourcing in Evidence, below).** Three of four proposed data needs are buildable now with no new extract:
- **M5 (net-of-transfer viability) — buildable, and better than originally scoped.** `02d::_entrant_fiscal` already computes the net fiscal calculation for the pool; it just isn't gating the viability decision yet (`_entrants_from_pool` still compares gross `y + s(y)` to `r`). **Bonus finding:** `01e_match_org_to_asec.py` already does ASEC household matching for the *employed* side (real `NCHILD` count, spouse income, children's ages); extending this match to the non-employed pool would both improve family-composition precision and hand M4 a genuine exclusion-restriction candidate (spouse income) far stronger than the current married/nchild dummy.
- **M7 (entrant hours) — buildable at the (cell) or (cell × wage-bracket) level.** Not at (state × cell): the smallest state/cell combination has 7 raw CPS rows (single mothers), too sparse for a stable draw.
- **M3/S2 (finer education, geography) — buildable now, no new extract.** All 16 IPUMS EDUC codes are already loaded (currently collapsed to 5 buckets); `STATEFIP` is fully populated with no sparsity concern even in the Heckman estimation sample's smallest state (1,304 rows).
- **M6 (fixed cost of work) — genuinely not buildable from data currently in this repo.** No childcare-expenditure or commute-time variable exists in CPS ORG or the ASEC extract; ATUS is registered in the dataset registry as a template but has never actually been pulled. This requires a new, permission-gated acquisition (ATUS is the leaner candidate; ACS `JWMNP` is a weaker proxy since non-employed persons have no observed commute). **This gap cuts against the "more entry" hope too:** without a fixed-cost term, a net-of-transfer viability test (M5) will still *overstate* how many non-employed people the subsidy makes viable, most acutely for single mothers — the cell where childcare need is highest.

## Scope note: 02b vs. 02d

`02b_behavioral_scenarios.py` (reduced-form headline cost band) and `02d_matching_simulation.py` (structural matching sim) both currently consume the same `eps_ext` table, but they play different roles: `02b` is explicitly a transparent "if the subsidy behaves like the EITC/CBO benchmark implies" band, which is a legitimate, disclosed use of the literature as a **literal target** — it is not claiming to be structural. `02d` claims to be a structural model of viability and bargaining, which is where mechanically re-deriving the EITC's own point estimate defeats the purpose of building the structural model. **Candidate resolution (for evaluation, not decided):** keep `02b`'s scenario table as an explicit "EITC/CBO benchmark" comparison case, and revise only `02d`'s calibration mechanism. This split is itself an open decision (see Clarity Status).

## Requirements

### MUST Have (Non-Negotiable)

- [ ] **M1** — Document, in the code and in `docs/`, the specific claim from the two source articles being operationalized (or not) at each point EITC elasticities are used, so a reader can see where the literature is a target vs. a prior vs. a bound. **Status: still to implement** (documentation task; unblocked).
- [ ] **M2 — RESOLVED (2026-07-08, after second literature-scout pass).** Mechanism: replace the equality-solved `λ_c` with a literature-informed **band**, per Candidate Direction 1. Band construction, resolved per-case:
  - **Women/single mothers:** central = EITC-implied; upper edge informed by Card & Hyslop (2005, Canadian SSP).
  - **Men/childless adults:** **central case stays anchored near the EITC-implied ~0.05** — no evidence found licenses moving the central estimate up. **The upper edge of the band is now meaningfully wider than the first pass recommended**, informed by MDRC's **Paycheck Plus NYC** (Miller et al. 2018) — a real RCT of a non-categorical, worker-side, earnings-linked credit reaching childless adults, which found employment gains up to 5.8 percentage points concentrated among the most disadvantaged men (noncustodial fathers, formerly incarcerated). **The lower edge is reinforced, not just EITC-derived**, by two independent findings: MDRC's Paycheck Plus **Atlanta** replication (Yang et al. 2022) found a **zero** effect for anyone, and the corrected reading of Bonin, Kempe & Schneider (2003) — now confirmed **worker-paid and non-categorical** (its own worked example is a childless single person), which still produced a null-to-negative male labor-supply result, via a mechanism distinct from categorical eligibility: **intra-household coordination** (a subsidy to one earner can reduce a partner's hours). Huttunen, Pirttilä & Uusitalo (2013) is now downweighted as a caution either way — confirmed employer-side, a demand-side hiring subsidy, not a clean analog to the worker-facing 80-80.
  - **Cross-cutting finding, disclosed as a modeling insight rather than promised as a build:** the consistent pattern across Paycheck Plus NYC and Parents' Fair Share (Miller & Knox 2001) is that responsiveness concentrates in a **specific disadvantaged/noncustodial subgroup within "men,"** not men as a demographic blend. CPS ORG/ASEC almost certainly cannot identify this subgroup (no noncustodial-parent or justice-involvement variable) — this has **not** been verified against the data-dictionary findings and should be treated as a low-probability build, not committed to. Disclose as a known limitation: the model's "men" cell will likely remain a blend that understates response for this subgroup and overstates it for the rest.
  - **New disclosed limitation (out of scope for this implementation pass):** household-coordination/cross-earner substitution effects (per the corrected Bonin finding) are not modeled anywhere in `02d` — individuals are treated independently. Document this explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.
  - Direction 2 (structural salience/no-cliff/non-categorical decomposition) remains **rejected** — the second pass reinforces rather than reverses this: even a real non-categorical, worker-side program (Bonin) did not straightforwardly produce more male entry, so "non-categorical" cannot be cleanly modeled as a standalone positive multiplier.
- [ ] **M3** — Extend the participation (selection) equation in `01h::_design`/`heckman_impute` to use age and education more flexibly (finer education categories and/or a more flexible age profile, possibly cell-interacted), while keeping both as **wage-equation regressors, not exclusion restrictions** (they affect wages, so they cannot be the participation-only shifters). **Status: confirmed buildable now**, no new extract (all 16 IPUMS EDUC codes already loaded).
- [ ] **M4** — Resolve the reservation-wage anchor question: the current imputed non-employed MPL median ($21.00) equals the employed median exactly, which is hard to reconcile with negative selection and suggests the Heckman correction (IMR coefficient +0.30) is weak. **Status: resolved to "do both, and add a real exclusion restriction."** (a) Strengthen the Heckman by adding `STATEFIP` and finer `EDUC` as wage/selection covariates (both confirmed buildable, no sparsity issue), and — the strongest lever — extend `01e_match_org_to_asec.py`'s existing ASEC household match from the employed pool to the non-employed pool to obtain **spouse income** as a genuine participation-only exclusion restriction (spouse income is a standard, defensible instrument; the current married/nchild dummy is weaker). (b) Retain a p25-anchored plausibility check on the resulting imputed non-employed wage distribution as a validation, not a replacement for the structural estimate.
- [ ] **M5** — Move the extensive-margin viability condition toward **net-of-tax-and-transfer income** using the `individual_schedules` PolicyEngine output already computed for the fiscal-cost side, rather than comparing gross wages. **Status: confirmed buildable now.** `02d::_entrant_fiscal` already computes this net calculation for the pool (same `family_type_key`/`state_code` key already used for incumbents) — the change is to gate `_entrants_from_pool`'s viability test on it instead of on gross `y + s(y) ≥ r`. Extending the ASEC match (per M4) also gives real household composition (actual `NCHILD` count, spouse income) in place of the coarse `0c`/`2c` bucket, improving the net-income calculation itself.
- [ ] **M6** — Add an explicit, disclosed, cell-specific **fixed cost of work** parameter (childcare, commuting) as a separate primitive from the multiplicative reservation markup, sourced from the literature (not fit to reproduce `eps_ext`). **Status: BLOCKED — genuinely not buildable from data in this repo.** No childcare-expenditure or commute-time variable exists in CPS ORG or the pulled ASEC extract. ATUS is registered in `Infrastructure/references/datasets/registry.yaml` as a template entry but has never been pulled. Requires a new, permission-gated data acquisition (ATUS recommended over ACS `JWMNP`, since non-employed persons have no observed commute) before this item can proceed — **out of scope for the initial implementation pass**; ship M5 with this limitation explicitly disclosed (net-of-transfer viability without a fixed-cost term still overstates entry likelihood, most for single mothers).
- [ ] **M7** — Replace the fixed `ENTRY_HOURS = 2000` assumption for entrants (`02d::_entrants_from_pool`) with an hours distribution informed by comparable observed low-wage incumbents by cell (addresses the intensive margin for entrants, which is currently unmodeled). **Status: confirmed buildable now**, at the (cell) or (cell × wage-bracket) level. Not at (state × cell) — the smallest state/cell cell has 7 raw rows, too sparse.
- [ ] **M8** — No implementation until this spec is approved by the user. This evaluation round's deliverable is the revised spec below, not code.

### SHOULD Have (Preferred)

- [ ] **S1** — Report the induced-entry margin as a **band** driven by the width of the literature-informed prior, not a single point, and disclose the band's provenance (which sources set the low/high ends). **Confirmed direction — folded into M2.**
- [ ] **S2** — Add geography (`STATEFIP`/region) to the wage/participation equations; both wages and reservation values vary meaningfully across states, and the data (`STATEFIP`) is already carried. **Confirmed buildable — folded into M4.**
- [ ] **S3** — Consider a job-finding-probability layer (rather than a pure viability threshold) if the literature-scout assessment surfaces a defensible, implementable friction parameter. **Not pursued** — literature-scout found no additional matching-theory literature extending the participation margin under an in-work subsidy beyond what `02d` already cites for bargaining; adding a friction layer now would be unsupported invention, not literature-informed.

### MAY Have (Optional, If Time)

- [ ] **A1** — A structural decomposition of the subsidy's participation effect into salience/no-cliff/non-categorical components. **Rejected** — no literature licenses separately estimable multipliers for these mechanisms (see Evaluation findings summary). Superseded by the M2 band approach.
- [ ] **A2** — Partial-pooling / Bayesian combination of an EITC-informed prior with wage-subsidy-specific evidence. **Deferred, not rejected** — could be a future refinement of the M2 band (e.g., weighting Card-Hyslop vs. EITC/Huttunen/Bonin evidence formally rather than presenting a simple band), but a simple disclosed band is sufficient for the initial implementation pass and is easier to audit.

## Candidate design directions — DECIDED

1. **Prior/band, not point target. — SELECTED (see M2).** EITC/CBO elasticities remain the central/lower anchor; Card & Hyslop (2005) informs a higher case for women/single mothers only; the near-zero men/childless-adult case is retained, not raised, per Huttunen-Pirttilä-Uusitalo (2013) and Bonin-Kempe-Schneider (2003).
2. **Structural features explicit (salience/no-cliff/non-categorical decomposition). — REJECTED.** No literature supports estimating these as separate multipliers; see Evaluation findings summary.
3. **Net-of-transfer reservation with fixed costs. — PARTIALLY SELECTED.** The net-of-transfer viability mechanism is adopted (M5, confirmed buildable). The fixed-cost-of-work term is deferred (M6, blocked on a new data acquisition) and its absence is explicitly disclosed as a limitation, not silently dropped.
