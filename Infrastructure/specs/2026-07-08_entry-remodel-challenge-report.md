## Executive summary

The prior session's **direction** survives the challenge: replace the equality-solved EITC calibration with a literature-informed band, move viability to a net-of-transfer basis, strengthen the imputation, and keep the men/women central anchors where the evidence puts them. Its **evidence conclusions** mostly survive — and are now far stronger, because every load-bearing number that was `[unverified]` has been pinned to a primary source this session.

But **four load-bearing implementation choices in the draft plan are wrong and were caught before any code was written**, and **one factual feasibility claim from the prior session's data pass is false**:

1. The proposed net-of-transfer gate **breaks the model's own calibration identity** (calibrate gross, gate net), with a perverse skew that silently shrinks the single-mothers cell most (ST-1, HIGH).
2. The proposed exclusion restriction — spouse income obtained by extending the ASEC nearest-neighbor match — is **econometrically vacuous** (a function of the same covariates already in the selection equation, plus matching noise; identification would collapse to IMR nonlinearity, concealed rather than disclosed) (ST-5/ST-6, HIGH).
3. **The ASEC microdata the plan proposed reusing is not on disk at all** — `data/external/` holds only the DDI XML; there is no `asec_earners_*.parquet` or `org_asec_matches.parquet` anywhere in the tree. The prior feasibility finding "buildable now with no new extract" is false for every ASEC-dependent item. The superior replacement — a **real within-household spouse link inside CPS ORG itself** (SERIAL/PERNUM/RELATE rosters; 96.6% of married-spouse-present adults linkable; spouse EMPSTAT in all rotations) — requires no acquisition and was missed by the prior pass.
4. The band mechanism's per-person rank `u` is assigned by **row order**, which is deterministically increasing in survey year/month (verified from ingestion code) and plausibly clustered by geography — so *who* enters is a file-order artifact even though the cell aggregate is calibrated correctly (ST-10, HIGH).

Two further findings the prior workstream never surfaced, both bearing on whether the model over- or under-states entry:

- `01h`'s calibration target is **linear** (`eps × mean(g)`) while the project's own config argues, and `02b` implements, a **saturating** response for exactly this subsidy's large `g` — the linear form overstates the target share by ~33–50% at central-scenario gains (ST-11, HIGH).
- The Heckman prediction step sets the Mills term to **zero**, returning the unconditional mean and discarding the estimated negative selection (`ρσ = +0.296`) — this, not weak exclusion restrictions, most plausibly explains the pool-median-equals-employed-median red flag that motivated M4 (ST-7, HIGH). Fixing it lowers imputed MPL and raises gains/entry; fixing ST-11 lowers entry. **The two corrections cut in opposite directions; the net effect is unknowable until run.** That is the honest answer to the motivated-reasoning question (item 10 below).

**Calibration consequences (feed into W2b):**
- The men upper edge must use the **pooled +2.8 ns / Year-3 +5.8\*\*** range, not the 5.8 headline — and must be **diluted by the disadvantaged-subgroup's population share within the model's "men" cell** before conversion to a cell-level `eps_ext`. ### 2. Re-verify Huttunen/Bonin characterizations — **PRIOR SESSION CONFIRMED, with one weight adjustment**

Independent re-reads of IZA DP 4931 and DP 587 confirm every prior characterization: Huttunen is employer-side (verbatim "paid to the employer"; age>54, hours, wage-band conditions), null on employment, hours effect confined to part-time→full-time conversion in the industrial sector; Bonin is worker-paid, non-categorical (childless-single worked example), men net **negative** (−4,800/−7,500 vs women +42,100/+111,500, Tabelle 1), net supply +37,300–104,000, ~90% deadweight.

**Weight adjustment the prior session under-emphasized:** Bonin is an **ex-ante microsimulation** whose gendered result is generated by *assumed* elasticities (sourced to Schneider et al. 2002b) interacting with German benefit floors — it demonstrates the household-coordination *mechanism* coherently, but it is not ex-post evidence and should not be weighted as if it were. The ex-post evidence on the men question is the Paycheck Plus pair (split) and Parents' Fair Share; Bonin supports *modeling* the coordination channel (see item 9), not the level of the men band.

### 3. Net-of-transfer viability formula — **FLAWED-FIXABLE (ST-1 HIGH; ST-2/3/4 MEDIUM)**

The comparison itself (`NI(package) ≥ NI(r·h)`, the `NI(0)` terms canceling) is economically coherent *if* `r` is read as a gross-wage-equivalent reservation under the same schedule. But as proposed it *breaks the calibration identity*: `01h` solves λ so the **gross** viable share hits `eps×mean(g)`; re-gating in 02d on the **net** criterion means induced entry no longer equals the model's own anchor even at the central point — and the wedge is largest for single mothers (highest EMTRs from benefit phase-outs), i.e., the calibration is silently violated most in the highest-elasticity cell. **Fix: calibrate the λ-bisection itself on the net criterion** (second-best: decouple and mandatorily report the per-cell calibrated-vs-realized wedge). Additional subtleties the plan missed, all real:
- **ST-2:** EITC/CBO participation elasticities are net-return concepts; the calibration stimulus `mean(g)` and the W2b unit conversions must move to the same net basis as the gate, or the band is internally inconsistent with its own anchors.
- **ST-3:** `NI(0)` does capture means-tested benefits at zero income (verified in `_ni_adjusted`/`_entrant_fiscal`), but misses **UI** for the unemployed subset (biases entry up and fiscal savings down — the two do not cancel), uses an **annual** schedule against a **monthly** nonemployment state, and implicitly treats the subsidy as taxable/countable income — a policy-design assumption that must be stated (the source articles' "no phase-out" framing suggests the opposite).
- **ST-4:** `NI()` is non-monotone (ACA/Medicaid/SNAP cliffs), so 02d's existing "pay up to clear r" repair can cross a cliff and flip viability; a net-basis clearing wage needs a small grid search over `[w_e, y]` plus a reported cliff-diagnostic count — which is substantively interesting in its own right, since the articles' no-cliff argument is *about* exactly this.

### 4. Spouse income as exclusion restriction — **OVERTURNED, three independent ways**

(a) **Vacuous as constructed (ST-5/6):** with `wage_decile` undefined for the non-employed, the NN match runs on {marital, nchild, age, sex, educ} — a subset of the selection covariates — so matched spouse income is `f(X_sel)` + matching arbitrariness; identification collapses to IMR nonlinearity (Puhani 2000), and the regressor would be constructed differently by the selection outcome `D` itself (employed rows matchable on wage decile, non-employed not), mechanically predicting selection. No version of the matched variable works as an instrument. (b) **Blocked anyway:** the ASEC microdata is absent from the repo; `01d`'s earner file (were it rebuilt) keeps only the highest-wage head/spouse earner per household and drops no-earner households entirely — matching *non-employed* people into it would bias spouse income upward by construction. (c) **The binding weakness is elsewhere (ST-7):** `heckman_impute` predicts the pool with the Mills term set to zero — the unconditional mean — discarding the negative selection the estimated `ρσ=+0.296` implies. The selection-consistent nonemployed imputation is `Xβ + ρσ·(−φ/(1−Φ)) < Xβ`. This largely explains the $21.00 = $21.00 red flag and must be fixed *before* judging whether any new exclusion restriction "worked."

**Replacement (feasible now, no acquisition):** real in-ORG spouse link — RELATE head↔spouse pairing links **96.56%** of married-spouse-present adults (94.93% within the pool); spouse `EMPSTAT` is populated in **all rotations**; spouse weekly earnings exist for the MISH 4/8 quarter of married pool rows. Use **spouse-employed × married** plus **own-child-under-5** (derivable essentially exactly for the 56% of the pool who are heads/spouses; household-level under-5 flag for the rest, over-attributing by ~1.3pp) as the Mroz-lineage participation shifters — *with the assortative-mating and motherhood-penalty caveats disclosed, and a Puhani-style sensitivity (Heckman vs. OLS imputation vs. conditional-Mills variants)*. The honest documentation position: no bulletproof exclusion restriction exists in these data; the current code's `married`/`nchild` exclusions already have documented direct wage associations (marriage premium, motherhood penalty — ST-8). Precondition the plan missed: `nonemployed_pool.parquet` carries **no person identifier** — `01h` must add YEAR/MONTH/SERIAL/PERNUM row keys before any link can join back.

### 5. Band construction / rank reuse — **MECHANISM CONFIRMED SOUND; one HIGH defect in the rank source**

Reusing one per-person rank across the three band edges is the **common-random-numbers principle** — correct for parameter/scenario uncertainty, eliminating Monte Carlo churn and yielding nested viable sets ("a higher elasticity means a superset of the same people enter"). The draft plan's verification step 1 is wrong in a helpful direction: pointwise monotonicity of the three reservation columns **is guaranteed** under shared `u`; assert it as a test. Required disclosure: the band represents `eps_ext` scenario uncertainty only — not sampling error, not markup-distribution-form uncertainty (ST-9).

The defect is the rank's **source** (ST-10): `u = (arange+0.5)/n` in row order. Ingestion writes partitions in IPUMS delivery order with no reshuffle (verified in `00a`/`01a`), so `u` is increasing in survey year/month **with certainty** (2025 respondents systematically get lower markups than 2026 respondents) and plausibly clustered by state within months (unverified — no pandas-capable interpreter in this environment). Aggregate shares calibrate correctly; *composition* — which feeds 02d's state- and family-keyed fiscal schedules — is a file-order artifact, deterministic and therefore invisible to the plan's determinism check. **Fix:** rank a hash of the stable person ID (preferred — invariant to reordering and pool growth) or a seeded within-cell permutation; reuse the same fixed `u` across edges. The docstring's "drawn INDEPENDENT of g" claim is false as written and becomes true under the fix.

### 6. `other_women` default — **OVERTURNED as stated**

The claim "no wage-subsidy-specific anchor exists" is wrong; the evidence was scattered across program types and turns on the **assessment unit**:
- The married-women *negatives* in the EITC/WFTC/PPE-married literature (Eissa & Hoynes 2004: ~−1pp LFP, verified; Brewer et al. 2006 WFTC: −0.57pp mothers-in-couples, verified; Stancanelli 2008 PPE-married: ~−3pp, verified) run through **household-income assessment** — a channel the individually-wage-assessed 80-80 does not have. These must never be imported into this cell.
- What transfers is the smaller **intra-household coordination/income-effect** channel (Bonin mechanism; Blau & Kahn 2007 show the cross-elasticity shrank 38–47% by 2000) — capping how far *below* the EITC default the truth plausibly sits (near zero, not negative).
- Individual-assessment evidence points **up**: Stancanelli's within-paper contrast (married −3pp vs. cohabiting/individually-taxed women **positive**); Francesconi, Rainer & van der Klaauw 2009 (≈0 average for married mothers even under a household test; positive where partner not working); and the now-verified **Paycheck Plus NYC women's** +4.6pp\*\*\* (Y2) / +3.2pp\*\*\* (pooled) — the only experimental estimate for childless single women — with Atlanta's null as counterweight.

**Recommendation:** keep central 0.20 (inside McClelland-Mok's married-women 0–0.3); widen the lower edge to ~0.05 (coordination channel + Atlanta; not zero or negative); retain upper 0.40, now with citable wage-subsidy anchors; relabel provenance ("anchored both sides", not "EITC-only"). Residual disclosed gap: no study isolates married women under a purely individual subsidy with no household ceiling. Four new sources cataloged (Eissa-Hoynes 2004; Blau-Kahn 2007; Brewer et al. 2006; Francesconi et al. 2009).

### 7. Fixed cost of work — **CONFIRMED, with understated in-repo options restored**

The core conclusion stands: no childcare/commuting **expense** measure exists in-repo, and a dollar-valued fixed-cost primitive (M6) still requires a new acquisition. Refinements: (i) the ASEC SPM expense variables (SPMCHXPNS, SPMCAPXPNS) are ASEC-only and were never pulled — an ATUS pull is not the only unblock path; a small ASEC re-pull is an alternative; (ii) the prior pass **understated** what exists now: own-child-under-5 (derivable from rosters), METFIPS metro size, and presence of another non-employed adult in the household are usable as *disclosed, crude need-shifters* — covariates that shift a fixed-cost prior by group, not measurements of it; (iii) NCHLT5 and SPLOC are available in basic monthly on a marginal re-pull. M6 deferral stands; the rev2 plan adds the need-proxy covariates and the re-pull menu.

### 8. 02b/02d scope split — **CONFIRMED, with three conditions**

Publishing a labeled reduced-form benchmark (02b) beside a structural estimate (02d) is standard practice *if*: (i) a **reconciliation table** attributes the wedge between the two entry numbers to its four sources — population (up-weighted incumbents vs. real pool), response form (saturating vs. currently linear), stimulus basis (gross vs. net), and elasticity source (ST-12); (ii) the **fallback coupling is fixed**: `02d::_entrants_synthetic` still reads `cfg["behavioral"]["scenarios"]["central"]` — under the plan, the model's entry margin would depend on *different config namespaces depending on whether a parquet file exists on disk* (ST-13); (iii) 02b's scenarios are **relabeled** as benchmarks and the `central` name collision across the two namespaces is avoided (ST-14).

### 9. Household coordination — **OVERTURNED: from "defer entirely" to "feasible as a disclosed sensitivity now"**

With the real spouse link (item 4), a crude adjustment is buildable with zero new data: flag pool members whose linked spouse is employed (~66% of linkable married pool rows) and report a **sensitivity row** that down-weights or zeroes their induced entry, bounding the Bonin coordination channel. It should be a *reported sensitivity, not a central-case adjustment*: no transportable magnitude exists (Bonin is model-based and German-institution-specific; Blau-Kahn shows the channel shrinking), and the spouse-employment selection covariate (item 4 fix) already internalizes part of the channel through participation propensity. The rev2 plan adds this as a small workstream.

### 10. Motivated-reasoning check — **PRIOR CONCLUSIONS PASS; the framing carried a directional prior; the verified record now cuts both ways**

- The prior session's *outcome* resisted the advocacy-favorable pull: it kept the men central at the EITC anchor when the assembled evidence would not support raising it, and it correctly elevated Bonin as a caution. That is the opposite of motivated reasoning in the conclusion.
- The *framing*, however, was directional throughout — "the EITC cap understates the subsidy's entry effect" — and nobody asked the mirror question: **does the model overstate entry?** This session found two structural reasons it may (linear-vs-saturating calibration, ST-11: ~33–50% overstatement of the target share at central gains) and one reason it may understate (Mills=0 imputation overstates MPL, understating gains, ST-7). These cut in opposite directions; the net direction is unknowable until run. Both fixes are correctness fixes, not tuning — implementing them and letting the number land where it lands is the correct posture.
- The now-verified evidence also disciplines the one place the prior session *did* drift optimistic: the men upper edge was justified by "up to 5.8pp" — which is a Year-3-only, stars-only estimate whose pooled counterpart (+2.8pp) is not significant, from one of two sites, for a disadvantaged sub-slice that must be diluted to the cell level, against a second site's precise null. A defensibly constructed men upper edge is therefore likely to be **modest**, and the preliminary SSP arithmetic (item 1) suggests the women's upper anchor may sit near the current central rather than above it. Net: the verified record, followed neutrally, points toward **narrower, not wider, upside bands** than the prior session's qualitative language implied — while the structural fixes leave the central level genuinely uncertain in both directions. That is what "following the evidence" looks like here, and it should be stated plainly in the methodology doc.
