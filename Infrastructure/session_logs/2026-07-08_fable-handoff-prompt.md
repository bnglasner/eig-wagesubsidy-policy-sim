=== PROJECT AND ORIGIN ===
Repo: eig-wagesubsidy-policy-sim (the 80-80 Rule wage-subsidy simulation). Read `PROJECT.md` first per repo convention, then `Infrastructure/GUARDRAILS.md`, `Infrastructure/AI_WORKFLOW.md`, and `.claude/agents/orchestrator.md`.

The policy: an 80-80 wage subsidy that fills 80 percent of the gap between a worker's employer-paid wage and a target set at 80 percent of the national median hourly wage. It is proposed as superior to the EITC for pulling nonworkers into the labor force, per two of the project's own published pieces (its own policy advocacy, not neutral third-party literature):
- https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/how-to-end-low-wage-work-forever
- https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/how-to-end-low-wage-work-forever-4a4

These articles argue the EITC's demonstrated labor-supply effect (strong for single mothers, near-zero for men/childless adults, citing Meyer 2002) results from the EITC's own institutional design: annual lump-sum payment, formula complexity, a phase-out cliff, and categorical (child-conditioned) eligibility. The 80-80 subsidy is explicitly designed to remove all of these frictions (paid every paycheck, transparent formula, no cliff, non-categorical / job-and-hour-specific).

=== THE TENSION THAT STARTED THIS WORKSTREAM ===
The repo's structural non-employment-entry model (`code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py::assign_reservation`) calibrates how many non-employed people the subsidy induces into work by solving, per demographic cell (single_mothers / other_women / men), for a reservation-wage markup rate such that induced-entry share EXACTLY EQUALS `eps_ext_c x mean(gain)`, where `eps_ext` is taken directly from EITC/CBO participation-elasticity estimates. Current config values (`code/00_setup/00_config.py`, `cfg["behavioral"]["scenarios"]`):
- lower: single_mothers 0.25, other_women 0.10, men 0.00
- central: single_mothers 0.50, other_women 0.20, men 0.05

This mechanically caps the wage subsidy's modeled entry effect at the EITC's own demographic pattern -- including near-zero for men -- even though the whole point of the policy is that it should not be limited by the EITC's specific frictions. `02d_matching_simulation.py`'s wage-bargaining side already uses rich non-EITC structural theory (Mortensen-Pissarides, Hosios, Rothstein, Flinn, Hall-Milgrom); only the entry TRIGGER was 100 percent EITC-derived.
