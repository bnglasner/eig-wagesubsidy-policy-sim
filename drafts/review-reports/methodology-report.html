Methodology Report

Econometric design concerns — err on side of flagging
Project: eig-wagesubsidy-policy-sim Reviewed: 2026-07-08 Files: 7 files across Python Reviewer: AI review agent (methodology-reviewer)

Executive Summary

Overall Methodological Risk: HIGH
This is a policy microsimulation of the EIG "80-80" wage subsidy, not a causal-inference study, so the DiD / RD / IV / matching checklists do not apply and the report is calibrated accordingly. The static eligibility-and-cost accounting on CPS ORG is sound and correctly survey-weighted (EARNWT for employed earners, WTFINL for the non-employed pool). The methodological risk is concentrated in the extensive-margin entry model. Review identified 15 concerns (1 HIGH, 9 MEDIUM, 5 LOW). The one HIGH concern is that the Heckman selection-corrected MPL imputation — which sets each non-employed person's potential wage and therefore who is eligible and inducible — is applied through a conditional Mills prediction whose validity rests on an exclusion restriction the authors themselves disclose does not exist, and that choice moves the pool median wage from roughly $21.5 (unconditional) to $16.3 (conditional), swinging the share below the $16.80 target. The MEDIUM tier centers on the transported-elasticity calibration, the fact that the structural matching model reproduces that calibrated entry count by construction, the definition of the "national median wage," and several survey-data pitfalls (unweighted estimation, topcoding, deflator/vintage consistency). The team's disclosure discipline is unusually strong; most concerns are prompts to confirm deliberate choices and to report the sensitivity band prominently rather than evidence of error.

MR-001
Heckman selection correction lacks a valid exclusion restriction; the used "conditional Mills" MPL drives eligibility and entry
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Causal Inference
HIGH
Relevant Code
Xsel = _design(est, wage_eq=False)   # selection adds: married, nchild, has_child,
probit = sm.Probit(D, Xsel.to_numpy()).fit(...)   #   spouse_employed_married, child_under5
...
xb_cond = xb + lam_coef * lam0        # E[log w | X, D=0] (ST-7) — the USED imputation
mpl_cond = np.maximum(FED_MIN, np.exp(xb_cond) * smear)
# DISCLOSED (methodology doc #3.7): no bulletproof exclusion restriction exists in
# these data (assortative mating; motherhood penalty; marriage premium).
Why This Is a Concern
The two-step Heckman corrects the wage equation for non-random selection into employment. Identification of the selection correction requires at least one variable that shifts participation but not the offered wage (the exclusion restriction). The instruments here — spouse-employed × married, own-child-under-5, married, nchild — are the conventional Mroz-lineage shifters, and each has a well-documented direct association with wages (assortative mating, the motherhood wage penalty, the marriage premium). The code explicitly discloses this. When the exclusion restriction fails, the selection correction is identified only off the nonlinearity of the inverse Mills ratio, which is fragile and can produce large, poorly-pinned adjustments (Puhani 2000; Heckman 1979). Here the choice is consequential: the diagnostics show the conditional prediction moves the pool median MPL from about $21.5 (Mills=0) and $26.3 (plain OLS) down to $16.3, which in turn sets the weighted share below the $16.80 target at 55.9 percent. Because the imputed MPL determines who is eligible, who is "reachable," and each person's net return to work, essentially every downstream headline (eligible counts, induced entry, cost) inherits this unidentified correction. The team runs a Puhani-style sensitivity (all three imputations printed), which is the right instinct — but only the conditional variant flows to disk and downstream.
What to Check
  • Confirm whether any covariate can be defended as excludable from the wage equation; if not, state plainly that the correction is functional-form-identified.
  • Propagate all three imputations (conditional / Mills=0 / plain OLS) through 02d as a reported band, not just the conditional point, so readers see the full swing in eligible counts and cost.
  • Report the pool MPL distribution and share-below-target under each imputation side by side in the paper (they are already computed in the diagnostics JSON).
  • Consider a bounding exercise (e.g., Lee bounds or a Manski-style range) as a design that does not depend on the exclusion restriction.
  • Sensitivity-test the log-normal smearing factor (currently 1.17) and the FED_MIN floor, which jointly shift the level of every imputed wage.

MR-002
Extensive-margin elasticities are transported from EITC/SSP/Paycheck Plus to a very different budget-set geometry
code/00_setup/00_config.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
"eps_ext_band": {
    "lower":   {"single_mothers": 0.25, "other_women": 0.05, "men": 0.00},
    "central": {"single_mothers": 0.50, "other_women": 0.20, "men": 0.05},
    "upper":   {"single_mothers": 0.65, "other_women": 0.40, "men": 0.15},
}
# IMPORTANT — these are TRANSPORTED, ex-ante assumptions. No elasticity has been
# estimated for a per-hour wage-FILL design ... g can exceed 1.0 near the minimum wage.
Why This Is a Concern
The entire induced-entry result rests on participation elasticities estimated for EITC-type in-work credits and demonstrations (SSP, Paycheck Plus) and applied to the 80-80 wage-fill, which delivers proportional gains (g) far larger than the marginal variation those elasticities were estimated on. A constant-elasticity response applied linearly over such large g would over-extrapolate; the code mitigates this with a saturating Michaelis-Menten form and a disclosed band. This is an untestable external-validity assumption that materially affects a headline (induced entry), so it should be surfaced as a primary caveat rather than a footnote. The disclosure in the config and 02b's closing note is exemplary; the flag is to ensure the band, not a central point, is what gets quoted, and that the extensive elasticity for men (near zero in the literature) is not implicitly given more weight than the evidence supports.
What to Check
  • Ensure every reported entry/cost figure carries the lower–upper band, never a lone central value.
  • State the linear-elasticity-overstates-response caveat prominently, with the ceiling-saturation form as the correction.
  • Confirm the cell weights (~41% men, ~48% other women, ~11% single mothers) and that near-zero male elasticities are not diluted by pooling.
MR-003
Structural matching model reproduces the calibrated entry count by construction, not as an independent prediction
code/02_descriptive_analysis/02d_matching_simulation.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
viable = req <= gnb * (1.0 + gnet) * (1.0 + 1e-9)   # calibration-identical max-package gate
# "Viability at the MAX package (w = y) reduces to the closed form ... identical to the
#  calibration condition, so induced entry reproduces the calibrated target by construction."
Why This Is a Concern
The Mortensen-Pissarides / Nash-bargaining machinery in 02d disciplines the wage split and fiscal accounting of entrants, but the number of entrants is set to equal the target share calibrated in 01h from the transported elasticities. The docstring states this explicitly. The risk is presentational: the "structural search-and-matching simulation" framing can read as if the entry count is an independent structural finding, when it is the transported-elasticity assumption re-expressed. The reconciliation note (entry_reconciliation.parquet) is the right guardrail; it should be reflected in the prose so the count is not double-credited as both a calibration input and a model output.
What to Check
  • State in the text that induced entry is calibrated, and that the matching model contributes wage incidence and fiscal split, not the entry count.
  • Keep the 02b-vs-02d wedge attribution attached to any side-by-side entry numbers.
  • Consider a genuinely structural entry margin (surplus threshold with a free reservation distribution) as a contrast to show how much the count is assumption-driven.

MR-004
Selection indicator conflates non-employment with employed-but-wage-unobserved
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Regression Specification
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
org["obs_wage"] = np.where(org["is_employed"] & np.isfinite(org["hwage"]), org["hwage"], np.nan)
...
D = est["obs_wage"].notna().to_numpy().astype(int)   # selection = 1 iff a valid wage observed
Why This Is a Concern
The probit models the probability of observing a wage, but D=0 pools two very different groups: the genuinely non-employed and employed persons whose hourly wage is missing or invalid (salaried workers where hours are missing, sentinel-stripped wages, hours > 99 set to NaN). Treating employed-with-unobserved-wage as "non-selected" mislabels a nontrivial share of participants as non-participants, biasing the participation probit and hence the Mills ratio that corrects the wage equation. With the employed-earner share at 0.64, roughly a third of the estimation sample is D=0, and some of that is measurement rather than non-participation. This is a selection-model specification issue (Little & Rubin 2002 on distinguishing missingness mechanisms), separate from the exclusion-restriction concern in MR-001.
What to Check
  • Quantify how many D=0 records are actually employed (EMPSTAT in {10,12}) but wage-unobserved.
  • Decide whether the selection equation should model employment vs. wage-observation, and align D accordingly.
  • If wage-observation is the intended selection event, ensure the interpretation of the correction reflects that (imputing to non-observers, not to non-workers).

MR-005
"National median wage" benchmark excludes salaried workers, lowering the eligibility threshold
code/00_setup/00_config.py · Sample & Data
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
"ws_median_hourly_wage": 21.00,  # median hourly wage among hourly workers (excludes salaried)
"ws_target_wage":        16.80,  # 0.80 * 21.00
# org_target_wage.json: {"median_wage": 21.0, "target_wage": 16.8, "n_source_rows": 64576}
Why This Is a Concern
The policy is described as filling toward 80 percent of the "national median wage," but the median that anchors the target ($21.00 → target $16.80) is computed only over hourly-paid ORG earners, excluding salaried workers who earn more on average. The hourly-worker median is therefore well below a full-workforce median, which lowers the target and narrows (or shifts) who is eligible and how large the per-worker subsidy is. This is a definitional choice that materially affects the headline threshold, so it should be named explicitly and its sensitivity shown. It also interacts with 02e, whose take-up denominator is likewise the hourly, wage-observed, non-self-employed workforce — a defensible but non-obvious universe.
What to Check
  • State the median's universe (hourly-paid ORG earners) wherever "median wage" appears.
  • Report the target and cost under an all-worker median (including salaried, e.g., from ASEC or the full ORG earnings series) as a sensitivity.
  • Confirm the $16.80 target consumed downstream always comes from org_target_wage.json, not the hard-coded fallback, when the extract changes.
MR-006
Repeated person-months treated as independent; two-year pooling window undocumented
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Sample & Data
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
parts = sorted(raw_dir.glob("year=*/part-0.parquet"))[-2:]   # last 2 years, no stated rationale
...
"weight": (nonemp["WTFINL"] / n_months).to_numpy(),   # person-months averaged to a stock
# docstring: "one row per non-employed person 16-64" — but no CPSIDP dedup is applied
Why This Is a Concern
Two issues compound here. First, the CPS 4-8-4 rotation means the same individual appears in multiple monthly partitions; the WTFINL / n_months divisor correctly rescales the population total to an average monthly stock, but the Heckman probit and OLS treat each person-month as an independent observation, overstating the effective sample and (were SEs used) understating them, and the pool is one row per person-month, not per person as the docstring claims. Second, the [-2:] restriction to the two most recent years carries no comment explaining why two years (the MR-SA1 undocumented-restriction standard). Because no inferential SEs are drawn from the Heckman fit, the independence issue is second-order for point imputation, but the person vs. person-month ambiguity (MR-SA3) can distort cell composition and the calibration weights.
What to Check
  • Add a comment justifying the two-year window (sample size vs. currency trade-off).
  • Decide whether the pool should be deduplicated to one row per CPSIDP, or relabel it as person-months.
  • If any SE or uncertainty is ever reported off these regressions, cluster by CPSIDP.
MR-007
Unmapped STATEFIP silently assigned to Texas, contaminating state tax/transfer schedules
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Sample & Data
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
org["state_code"] = org["STATEFIP"].map(_FIPS).fillna("TX")
# _FIPS covers 50 states + DC; combined codes 61-90 and 99 (state not identified) -> "TX"
Why This Is a Concern
Any STATEFIP value not in the 51-entry map — including IPUMS combined-state codes (61–90) and "state not identified" (99) — is silently coerced to Texas rather than flagged. State drives the PolicyEngine schedule lookup (state income tax, Medicaid/TANF/SNAP parameters), so a misassigned state applies the wrong tax-and-transfer environment to those persons. Texas also has no state income tax, so the fallback is not neutral; it systematically understates state-tax interactions for the affected rows. 02a's schedule fallback ladder likewise ends at TX. If the extract contains only clean 1–56 codes this is harmless, but the silent default should be verified, not assumed.
What to Check
  • Count rows hit by the .fillna("TX") and 02a's TX schedule fallback; if non-trivial, handle explicitly.
  • Prefer dropping or separately flagging unidentified-state records over a silent TX default.
  • Confirm the ORG extract carries only clean 2-digit FIPS 01–56 for the analysis window.

MR-008
Heckman probit and wage OLS are unweighted while the target is a population-representative pool
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Dataset Variable Usage
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
probit = sm.Probit(D, Xsel.to_numpy()).fit(disp=0, maxiter=200)   # no weights
ols    = sm.OLS(y, Xw.to_numpy()).fit()                            # no EARNWT / WTFINL
Why This Is a Concern
Everything downstream is survey-weighted (EARNWT for employed earners, WTFINL for the pool, which the registry confirms are the correct weights), but the estimation that produces the imputed MPL is unweighted. For a conditional-mean predictor of a population quantity, unweighted estimation can be defensible on efficiency grounds when the model is correctly specified (Solon, Haider & Wooldridge 2015) — but that is a judgment that should be stated and tested, not left implicit, especially given the ORG earner design is exactly the case where sampling probabilities differ across the wage distribution the model must span. This is the MR-VU1 pattern (a recognized survey used in a regression without weights); it is graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the population aggregates are properly weighted and the regression is a predictor, not an inference target.
What to Check
  • Re-estimate the probit and wage OLS with EARNWT (wage equation) / WTFINL (selection) and compare the imputed MPL distribution.
  • If unweighted estimation is retained, cite the Solon–Haider–Wooldridge rationale in a comment.
  • Confirm the smearing factor is computed consistently under whichever weighting is chosen.
MR-009
Estimation wages strip sentinels but do not apply the topcode correction the main pipeline uses
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Dataset Variable Usage

Why This Is a Concern
The registry records that the main pipeline applies Pareto imputation above the EARNWEEK topcode plus a MISH-8 phase-in harmonization; 01h re-derives employed wages with sentinel stripping only. The employed wages that train the Heckman wage equation are therefore censored differently from the main analysis series, which can bias the upper part of the fitted wage distribution and the smearing factor. Because the subsidy targets low wages, topcoding is less critical for eligibility itself, but the wage-equation slope and the log-normal retransformation are estimated over the full range. Relatedly, CPS/ASEC income variables (INCWAGE, INCTOT) are topcoded per the registry (MEDIUM pitfall) and should be acknowledged wherever income enters. This is a methodology-consistency flag; whether the divergence is a bug belongs in the code report.
What to Check
  • Decide whether 01h should apply the same topcode treatment as 01a/01b for the estimation sample, and document the choice.
  • Check sensitivity of the IMR coefficient and smearing factor to topcode handling.
  • Acknowledge topcoding wherever ASEC income (INCWAGE/INCTOT) informs the analysis.

MR-011
Saturation ceilings (1.50 extensive, 1.40 intensive) are judgment bounds without a stated sensitivity
code/00_setup/00_config.py · Causal Inference
LOW
Relevant Code
"saturation": {"ceiling_ext": 1.50, "ceiling_int": 1.40},
# "Both are disclosed judgment bounds; sensitivity is via these values."
Why This Is a Concern
The Michaelis-Menten ceilings cap how far employment/hours can rise as the wage gain g grows large, and they bind precisely where the 80-80 delivers its largest gains — i.e., for the lowest-wage, highest-response workers. The values are disclosed as judgment calls, which is appropriate, but the results' sensitivity to them is not reported. Since the ceiling governs the top of the induced-entry response, a modest change could move the upper band.
What to Check
  • Report induced entry and cost under alternative ceilings (e.g., 1.25 and 1.75 extensive).
  • Note what share of entrants are in the saturating (g large) region where the ceiling binds.

MR-014
Headline aggregates reported as point estimates with no sampling-uncertainty band
code/02_descriptive_analysis/02a_descriptive_stats.py · Standard Errors
LOW
Relevant Code
gross_cost_bn = (gross_subsidies * weights).sum() / 1e9
net_cost_bn   = (net_income_delta * weights).sum() / 1e9   # single point, no CI
Why This Is a Concern
Cost and worker-count aggregates are survey-weighted point estimates. CPS provides replicate weights for design-consistent variance estimation, and none are used, so the estimates carry no sampling-error band (Lumley 2010). The behavioral/matching band already conveys parameter uncertainty, but that is distinct from sampling uncertainty in the underlying counts. For a blog/interactive audience a full replicate-weight variance may be overkill, hence LOW, but a rough CI on the headline eligible-worker count would strengthen the numbers.
What to Check
  • Consider reporting a sampling-error band on eligible counts and gross cost (replicate weights or a design effect).
  • Distinguish sampling uncertainty from the behavioral parameter band in the write-up.
