Methodology Report

Econometric design concerns — err on side of flagging
Project: eig-wagesubsidy-policy-sim Reviewed: 2026-07-09 Files: 15 files across Python, R Reviewer: AI review agent (methodology-reviewer)

Executive Summary

Overall Methodological Risk: HIGH
This is a policy microsimulation, not a causal-identification study: static accounting on CPS ORG microdata, a reduced-form elasticity transport, a structural search-and-matching model, and a labor-force-entry model calibrated to EITC/CBO and experimental (SSP, Paycheck Plus) elasticities. Accounting outputs (eligibility, cost, distribution) are sound, and the survey-weight usage is correct per the dataset registry — EARNWT for earner wages/eligibility/cost and WTFINL for the non-employed pool, with the Heckman weighted as WTFINL-probit / EARNWT-wage-OLS, exactly the MR-VU2 convention (no MR-VU1/VU2 finding is raised). The two HIGH concerns are both about the behavioral half of the model and are design-inherent and extensively disclosed: (MR-001) the potential-wage imputation for non-workers is a Heckman two-step with no exclusion restriction, and (MR-002) the headline entry count is calibrated to transported elasticities rather than identified from the data — it reproduces the assumed target by construction. Both prior-review HIGH findings still stand. The new paid-hourly eligibility restriction resolves the earlier recipient/target/imputation frame inconsistency and shrinks the selection correction (ρσ ≈ +0.11), but introduces one new medium concern: the selection model is now a binary paid-hourly-vs-non-employed probit that omits salaried employment as a competing sector (MR-003). Remaining MEDIUM/LOW items concern researcher-degrees-of-freedom in the entry re-centering, the absence of any sampling-based uncertainty (only parameter bands are reported), reliance on data provenance rather than code to bound the analysis window, and repeated person-months treated as independent. Overall risk is HIGH because of the two disclosed identification limits on the flagship entry estimate — with the strong caveat that the authors label these as transported/calibrated and publish sensitivity bands rather than a false point estimate.

MR-001
Potential-wage imputation for non-workers has no exclusion restriction (Heckman identified off the IMR functional form)

Why This Is a Concern
The counterfactual wage a non-worker would earn is imputed with a two-step Heckman selection correction, but the "instruments" that shift selection (marriage, number of children, child under 5, spouse employment) are conventional Mroz-lineage shifters that plausibly enter the wage equation directly (assortative mating, marriage premium, motherhood penalty). With no valid exclusion restriction, the selection correction is identified only off the non-linearity of the inverse Mills ratio — a well-known fragility (Mroz 1987; Puhani 2000). The imputed potential-wage distribution drives the entire "reachability" of the subsidy (share of the non-employed below the $16.80 target), which then couples mechanically to the headline entry count. The authors disclose this candidly (appendix §4, "no clean instrument"), run three imputation variants (conditional / Mills=0 / plain OLS) plus a non-employment wage-penalty band in 02f, and note the correction is small on the paid-hourly frame (ρσ ≈ +0.11). Those bounds are the right response; the finding stands (carried from the prior review) because the identification limit is real and load-bearing for the flagship result.
What to Check
  • Confirm the imputation-variant band (02f) and the wage-penalty band {0, 10, 20%} are reported alongside the headline entry number in every venue that quotes 1.49M, not only in the appendix.
  • Consider a genuine exclusion restriction if any becomes available (e.g., local labor-demand shifters, non-labor income), and report its selection-equation strength.
  • State explicitly that the potential-wage distribution is an imputation, not an observation, wherever the "47.8% of the pool below target" figure appears.
  • Verify the three imputation variants actually converge on the paid-hourly frame as claimed (the earlier 7× spread was attributed to salaried contamination) and persist the variant spread in the diagnostics JSON.
MR-002
Induced-entry count is calibrated to transported elasticities, not identified; the headline reproduces the assumed target by construction
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py, code/02_descriptive_analysis/02d_matching_simulation.py · Causal Inference
HIGH
Relevant Code
# 01h assign_reservation_band: markups m_i ~ Exponential(lambda), lambda bisected
# so the weighted viable share hits a target derived from transported eps_ext:
target = min(0.90, np.average(_b.response_multiplier(eps, gr, _SAT_EXT) - 1.0, weights=wr))
m_edge[e][ridx] = _solve(target)      # solve lambda so realized share == target

# 02d _entrants_from_pool docstring:
#   "Viability at the MAX package (w = y) reduces to the closed form ... identical
#    to the calibration condition, so induced entry reproduces the calibrated
#    target by construction."
Why This Is a Concern
The reservation-wage markups m_i are not estimated — they are drawn from an exponential distribution whose scale is chosen so each demographic cell's viable-entry share equals a target computed from imported participation elasticities (EITC/CBO ranges; SSP and Paycheck Plus anchors) passed through a Michaelis-Menten saturating function with a judgment ceiling (1.5). By the authors' own statement, the entry count then "reproduces the calibrated target by construction." The 1.49M headline is therefore an assumption transported from other policies, not an effect estimated for the 80-80 design — no program with the 80-80's exact geometry (permanent, universal, per-paycheck, no phase-out) has been evaluated, and the extensive-margin elasticity is disputed at its foundation (Kleven 2024 ≈ 0 vs. Chetty 2012 vs. the CTC debate's 0.75). Transporting elasticities estimated on marginal variation to the 80-80's very large proportional gains (g often > 1 near the minimum wage) risks over-extrapolation even with the saturating form. The authors handle this exceptionally well — labeling every parameter as transported, publishing a 27-cell penalty×dispersion×elasticity grid (0.23–3.81M), a reduced-form vs. structural cross-check, an employment-stock base sensitivity, and precedent ladders. The finding stands (carried from the prior review) because the central number is a calibration, and readers must not mistake it for an identified causal quantity.
What to Check
  • Ensure the word "calibrated" (not "estimated") governs every presentation of the entry counts, and that the full 0.23–3.81M grid range is shown, not just the 1.49M point.
  • Confirm the saturating-response ceiling (1.5) and the exponential markup form are presented as disclosed judgment with attached sensitivity, since the count scales directly with them.
  • Verify the reduced-form benchmark (0.48M) and structural (1.02–1.49M) entry figures are always paired with the wedge attribution (entry_reconciliation.parquet), so the two are never quoted side-by-side as agreement.
  • Sensitivity-test the linear eps·g margin against the saturating form at the largest g values to quantify the over-extrapolation the config comments warn about.

MR-003
Paid-hourly restriction reduces the selection model to a binary paid-hourly-vs-non-employed probit, omitting salaried employment as a competing sector
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
org["obs_wage"] = np.where(org["is_paid_hourly_earner"], org["hwage"], np.nan)
# Estimation sample: non-employed OR paid-hourly earner. Salaried employed DROPPED.
est = org[org["MISH"].isin(_ORG_MISH) &
          (~org["is_employed"] | org["is_paid_hourly_earner"])].copy()
D = est["obs_wage"].notna()   # D=1 paid-hourly; D=0 = non-employed only (salaried removed)
Why This Is a Concern
The new paid-hourly eligibility restriction (PAIDHOUR==2) is now applied consistently to the recipient frame (01a), the take-up denominator (02e), and the wage-imputation frame (01h) — which correctly resolves the earlier recipient/target/imputation-frame inconsistency and is the right frame for a policy that prices the hourly labor market (appendix §4). The side effect is that the Heckman selection equation now models a binary choice — "paid-hourly earner" (D=1) vs. "non-employed" (D=0) — with salaried employment (≈44% of earners) dropped from the sample entirely. Employment is really a multinomial state (non-employed / paid-hourly / salaried), so the selection correction is estimated relative to a truncated universe, and every non-worker is imputed a counterfactual hourly-sector wage judged reachable against the $16.80 hourly target. A non-worker whose best realistic offer is a salaried job above the target is either mislabeled reachable (if their imputed hourly wage falls below target) or has their earning capacity understated. The direction of the resulting bias on reachability, and hence on entry, is not tested. This is the flip side of the frame-consistency improvement and is a genuinely new consequence of the restriction.
What to Check
  • Assess how many non-employed entrants would plausibly enter salaried rather than hourly jobs, and whether the hourly-frame imputation over- or under-states their reachability.
  • Consider a sensitivity in which the counterfactual employment sector (hourly vs. salaried) is modeled, or bound entry under a "some entrants take above-target salaried jobs" scenario.
  • Document that the selection probit's D=0 group is non-employed only (salaried excluded), so the estimated ρσ is not comparable to a probit that treats all employment as D=1.
  • Confirm the shrinkage of ρσ from ≈+0.30 to ≈+0.11 is attributable to the frame change and not to the sample truncation itself.
MR-004
Headline entry depends on disclosed-but-unidentified structural knobs (status-differentiated wage penalty, dispersion λ, markup form, status weights)
code/00_setup/00_config.py, code/02_descriptive_analysis/02g_entry_scenario_grid.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
"entry_recenter": {
    "evidence_central_status_penalty": {"unemployed":0.05,"nilf_other":0.10,
                                        "disabled":0.15,"retired":0.15},
    "evidence_central_lambda": 0.75, ...},
"offer_dispersion": {"lambda_central": 0.75, "band": [0.50, 1.00]},
"entry_status_weights": {"unemployed":5.0,"nilf_other":1.0,"disabled":0.15,"retired":0.15},
Why This Is a Concern
The headline was deliberately moved from the "conservative floor" (1.02M, zero non-employment wage penalty) to an "evidence-central" (1.49M) by applying a status-differentiated wage penalty averaging ≈10%. The direction is well justified (offer decay ≈0.8%/month is documented — Schmieder, von Wachter & Bender 2016; Krueger & Mueller 2016), but the penalty magnitude (5–15% by status), the dispersion scale (λ = 0.75, chosen to "net out" ≈0.10–0.15 log points of CPS measurement error), the exponential markup functional form, and the status entry-weights (5.0 for the unemployed vs. 0.15 for disabled/retired) are all researcher-selected. Each is defensible, but together they are degrees of freedom that move both the headline count and — for the status weights — the composition of who enters. The measurement-error subtraction that sets λ is itself an unverified assumption. The authors disclose all of these, publish the joint grid, and note the count is insensitive to the status weights while the composition is not. Flagged so the reader treats 1.49M as a chosen central within a wide band, not a data-driven point.
What to Check
  • Confirm the floor (1.02M) and the full grid range receive equal prominence with the 1.49M central in the brief, not only in the appendix.
  • Justify the ≈0.10–0.15 log-point CPS measurement-error figure that pins λ = 0.75, or show the headline under λ = 1.00 (no netting).
  • Report the entrant composition (unemployed/NILF/disabled/retired shares) under a uniform lottery as well, since the composition is acknowledged to depend on the disclosed status weights.
  • Verify the "evidence rules out a zero penalty" argument is not stretched to also justify the specific 10% magnitude, which remains judgment.
MR-005
No demand side, no displacement, no general-equilibrium wage effects beyond the bargaining split
code/02_descriptive_analysis/02d_matching_simulation.py · Causal Inference
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
# 02d: every viable match forms (S = y - r >= 0); entrants placed at the bottom
# of the wage ladder; incidence = bargaining share (1-beta) on NEW matches only
# when incumbent_wage_rigid=True. No labor-demand constraint, no displacement of
# incumbents by entrants, no equilibrium employment feedback.
Why This Is a Concern
The structural model sets employment purely by match viability: every worker-firm pair with non-negative surplus forms a job, with no labor-demand constraint and no possibility that ~1.5M entrants displace existing low-wage incumbents. For a program touching 15.9M incumbents plus ~1.5M entrants, equilibrium wage and employment feedback (beyond the segmented bargaining split) could materially change both cost and net job creation. The authors disclose this in appendix §8 and argue ~1.5M steady-state entrants against 6–7M monthly hires is unlikely to bind, and note it runs opposite to the conservative wage-penalty/dispersion settings (the "genuinely two-sided" framing). Flagged per the err-on-flagging rule: the absence of a demand side is a substantive assumption behind both the cost and entry numbers, not merely a modeling nicety.
What to Check
  • Bound the displacement channel: what share of modeled entry could substitute for incumbent hours/jobs at the bottom of the wage ladder?
  • State the no-demand-side assumption wherever cost-per-job comparisons with industrial policy appear, since those alternatives are evaluated in equilibrium.
  • Consider a simple labor-demand-elasticity stress test on aggregate entry, even if only as a disclosed bound.

MR-006
Analysis window enforced by input-data scope, not by any code date filter; 01a pools all partitions while 01h slices the last two
code/01_data_preparation/01a_data_ingest.py, code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Sample & Data
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
# 01a: loads EVERY partition present — no year/month filter at all
parts = sorted(panel_dir.glob("year=*/part-0.parquet"))
raw = pd.concat([pd.read_parquet(p) for p in parts], ignore_index=True)
median_wage = _weighted_median(ph["hourly_wage_epi"].values, ph["earnwt"].values)

# 01h: hard-coded positional slice to the last two year-partitions
parts = sorted(raw_dir.glob("year=*/part-0.parquet"))[-2:]
Why This Is a Concern
The draft states the analysis uses the 12 months May 2025–May 2026 and reports a $21.00 paid-hourly median. But 01a applies no date filter — it pools every partition it finds and computes the dynamic median, eligibility, and all of 02a over whatever window the input contains. The upstream EIG-Wage-Figure ORG panel spans 1982–present; if this repository's cps_org_panel ever contains more than the intended recent window, 01a would silently compute a multi-decade nominal median (mixing 1982 and 2026 dollars) with no error. Meanwhile 01h uses a positional [-2:] slice, so the employed side (01a) and the non-employed side (01h) can silently cover different windows if the partition set changes. The window is thus guaranteed only by data provenance (extract #580), not by the code — a replication and internal-consistency risk, and an undocumented implicit sample restriction (MR-SA1).
What to Check
  • Add an explicit YEAR/MONTH filter (or an assertion on the observed date range) in 01a so the intended window is enforced in code, not assumed from the input.
  • Make 01a and 01h read the same window (a shared config date range) rather than "all partitions" vs. [-2:].
  • Assert the computed median falls in a plausible nominal band ($20–$22) and fail loudly otherwise.
  • Record the exact months included in a run manifest so replication does not depend on which partitions happen to be on disk.
MR-007
Repeated person-month observations (CPS 4-8-4 rotation) treated as independent; no person-level clustering in estimation
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Sample & Data
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
est = org[org["MISH"].isin({4,8}) & (...)]     # both ORG rotations pooled
sm.GLM(D, Xsel, family=Binomial(Probit()), freq_weights=w_sel).fit()  # raw WTFINL
sm.WLS(y, Xw, weights=w_wage).fit()                                    # raw EARNWT
Why This Is a Concern
Under the CPS 4-8-4 rotation the same individual is interviewed in month-in-sample 4 and again in month-in-sample 8, so a person can appear twice in the pooled ORG estimation sample. The Heckman probit and wage WLS treat person-months as independent observations and use raw EARNWT/WTFINL as frequency weights (the /n_months normalization that converts to period-average population totals is applied only to the output pool weight, not to the estimation weights). This double-counts recurring persons and understates the effective information in the fit; it also violates the independence assumption behind any coefficient standard error. Because the pipeline reports no standard errors (see MR-008), the immediate consequence is confined to the imputation coefficients and to potential double-counting of the same person in the estimation sample, but it is a genuine unit-of-analysis / serial-correlation issue (MR-SA3).
What to Check
  • De-duplicate or cluster by CPSIDP (person) in the Heckman estimation, or restrict the estimation sample to one rotation per person.
  • If coefficient standard errors are ever reported or used, cluster them at the person (and ideally household) level.
  • Confirm the raw-weight frequency weighting in the probit/WLS does not double-count persons appearing in both MISH 4 and 8 within the window.

MR-008
No sampling-based uncertainty on any point estimate; bands are parameter sensitivity, not sampling variance
code/02_descriptive_analysis/02a_descriptive_stats.py (and 02b/02d/02g) · Standard Errors
MEDIUM
Relevant Code
total_workers_mn = weights.sum() / 1e6
gross_cost_bn    = (gross_subsidies * weights).sum() / 1e9
# point estimates only; no replicate weights, no bootstrap, no CI anywhere in 02a/02b/02d/02g
Why This Is a Concern
Every number derived from CPS microdata (15.88M eligible, $55.88B gross, state counts, take-up rates, the median wage itself) is a weighted point estimate with no confidence interval. The uncertainty bands the project publishes are parameter bands (elasticity, penalty, dispersion, β) — they do not capture CPS sampling error. CPS ORG provides replicate weights for variance estimation; they are not used, and no design-based bootstrap is performed (MR-VU3 replicate-weights pitfall; Lumley 2010, Complex Surveys). For a 12-month ORG sample, state-level counts (e.g., West Virginia's 105,000 eligible) and small-cell take-up rates carry non-trivial sampling error that is invisible to the reader, and precise figures ("$4,981 in Louisiana", "317,000 eligible") overstate precision.
What to Check
  • Attach sampling-error bounds (replicate-weight or design-based bootstrap) to at least the headline eligibility, cost, and median-wage figures.
  • Flag small-cell state and subgroup estimates (e.g., graduate-degree recipients, single-state counts) as imprecise, or suppress cells below a reliability threshold.
  • Distinguish clearly in the text between parameter-uncertainty bands and sampling-uncertainty bands so the $45–48B band is not read as a confidence interval.

MR-009
No summary-statistics / balance table for the Heckman and entry-pool estimation covariates
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py, code/02_descriptive_analysis/02a_descriptive_stats.py · Descriptive Analysis

# (age, education distribution, race, state, spouse-employed, child-under-5).
Why This Is a Concern
The project produces rich recipient-distribution tables, but there is no conventional summary-statistics table (N, mean, SD) for the variables entering the selection and wage equations or characterizing the non-employed pool. Because the entry result rests entirely on the imputation, a reader cannot audit whether the D=1 estimation sample and the D=0 pool are comparable on observables, how much missingness there is in the household-link shifters (spouse employment is unlinkable for ~5.1% of the married pool per 01i), or whether any covariate is degenerate within a cell. A summary/balance table comparing the employed paid-hourly estimation sample to the non-employed pool would make visible the selection the model is correcting for (MR-DA1, MR-DA6).
What to Check
  • Add a summary-statistics table (weighted N, mean, SD) for all Heckman covariates, separately for the D=1 paid-hourly estimation sample and the D=0 non-employed pool.
  • Report missingness/linkability rates for the household-link shifters (spouse_is_employed, own_child_under5) used in the selection equation.
  • Include a pre-imputation comparison of employed vs. non-employed observables so the direction of selection is visible independent of the model.

MR-010
Hourly-wage topcoding in the wage-equation upper tail not explicitly addressed in the imputation frame
code/01_data_preparation/01h_nonemployed_pool.py · Dataset Variable Usage
LOW
Relevant Code
hw = np.where(hw >= 999.99, np.nan, hw)   # only the NIU sentinel stripped
# the legitimate HOURWAGE topcode (~$99.99) is retained (not Pareto-adjusted),
# following EPI; the wage OLS is fit on this topcoded upper tail.
Why This Is a Concern
CPS hourly wages are topcoded (the legitimate HOURWAGE topcode ≈ $99.99 for the relevant era), and the 01b weekly-wage Pareto adjustment applies to salaried EARNWEEK, not to hourly HOURWAGE. The log-wage OLS in the Heckman is therefore fit on a topcoded upper tail, which can flatten the estimated returns to age/education and slightly bias the conditional-mean imputation. Impact is limited here because eligibility and the median ($21) sit well below the topcode and the decision-relevant imputation region is below the $16.80 target, but the topcoding is a registry-flagged CPS pitfall (cps-topcoded-income) that is not acknowledged for the hourly frame.
What to Check
  • Confirm the share of paid-hourly earners at/above the hourly topcode is negligible in this window; if not, consider a tail adjustment or acknowledge the flattening.
  • Note in the appendix that hourly topcoding is retained (per EPI) and argue why it does not affect below-target imputation.
